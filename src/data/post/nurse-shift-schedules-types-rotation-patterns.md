## Key Takeaways

- A nurse shift schedule sets which hours each nurse works (the shift) and which days she works them (the pattern), repeated over a set number of weeks, for example 4 or 6.
- Nursing shifts run 8 hours, three to a day, or 12 hours, two to a day. Full time is five 8-hour shifts (40 hours a week) or three 12-hour shifts (36 hours a week).
- A fixed schedule keeps a nurse on the same shift and the same days each week. A rotating schedule moves her between shifts or between days on a set pattern.
- Patterns differ most in their busiest week. In our worked example, a 2-2-3 pattern on 12-hour shifts averages 42 hours a week but alternates 60-hour and 24-hour weeks. Under the [federal 40-hour workweek](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/54-healthcare-overtime), that is 20 hours of overtime every other week.
- On 12-hour shifts, every other weekend fits inside a 36-hour week with no more than 2 days worked in a row. On 8-hour shifts, the same weekends need either a long run of days or uneven weeks.

Every nurse shift schedule rests on two choices: how long each shift is, and how the shifts repeat from week to week. Together they decide a nurse's weekly hours, her overtime and how many weekends she works. This guide sets out the shift lengths and rotation patterns side by side, with the hours worked out for one nurse on each, whether you build the schedule by hand or with [nurse scheduling software](/nurse-scheduling-software).

## What Is a Nurse Shift Schedule?

A nurse shift schedule is the plan that says which nurse works which shift on which day. It covers a set number of weeks, for example 4 or 6, and then the next period starts. A workable schedule keeps the unit covered around the clock, keeps each nurse's weekly hours under control, and shares nights, weekends and holidays fairly.

Two rules sit under every schedule at a critical access hospital. The first is a Medicare staffing rule. It requires that "a registered nurse, clinical nurse specialist, or licensed practical nurse is on duty whenever the CAH has one or more inpatients." That rule is [42 CFR 485.631(a)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/485.631). The second is federal overtime law. Hospital overtime is generally due after 40 hours in a workweek, as the Department of Labor's [Fact Sheet #54 on health care overtime](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/54-healthcare-overtime) explains.

In day-to-day terms, the manager drafts the period, works in leave and requests, and posts the schedule before the period starts. After that, callouts and swaps are handled as changes to the posted schedule.

## What Are the Typical Nursing Shift Lengths and Times?

The two standard nursing shift lengths are 8 hours and 12 hours. Eight-hour shifts split the day into three, for example 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., 3 p.m. to 11 p.m. and 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. Twelve-hour shifts split it into two, for example 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. and 7 p.m. to 7 a.m.

Full time on 8-hour shifts is five shifts a week, which is 40 hours. Full time on 12-hour shifts is three shifts a week, which is 36 hours. A fourth 12-hour shift in the same week takes a nurse to 48 hours, 8 hours past the 40-hour overtime line.

Some units use 10-hour shifts instead, four a week for 40 hours, with staggered start times. Our [free nurse schedule templates](/blog/free-nurse-schedule-templates) guide shows example times for all three lengths.

Which length suits your unit, including how many shifts each one leaves you to fill, is covered in our comparison of [8-hour vs 12-hour nursing shifts](/blog/8-hour-vs-12-hour-nursing-shifts).

## What Is the Difference Between Fixed and Rotating Nurse Shifts?

A fixed shift keeps a nurse on the same shift every time she works, such as always days or always nights, and often on the same days of the week. A rotating shift moves her between shifts, or between days of the week, on a set pattern. One schedule can hold both: fixed day nurses alongside a group that rotates.

Fixed shifts are the easiest to plan, and each nurse knows her hours months ahead. The weak spot is fixed nights. If nights are covered by a small fixed group, losing one of them leaves shifts that nobody else is used to working. Our guide to [night shift nurse schedules](/blog/night-shift-nurse-schedule-coverage) weighs permanent nights against rotating nights.

A separate question is who sets the pattern. In cyclical scheduling, one fixed pattern repeats cycle after cycle. In self-scheduling, nurses choose their own shifts within limits the manager sets. Our comparison of [cyclical scheduling and self-scheduling](/blog/cyclical-scheduling-vs-self-scheduling-small-hospital) covers when each fits a small hospital.

Read the busiest-week column first. The 2-2-3 runs two days on, two off, three on, then two off, two on, three off, on a 14-day loop. Laid out as in the table, week 1 has five 12-hour shifts, 60 hours, and week 2 has two, 24 hours. That averages 42 hours a week, but each 60-hour week carries 20 hours of overtime, so 80 hours over 8 weeks (20 × 4).

Where the loop starts matters too. Start the same 2-2-3 so the three-day blocks fall Wednesday to Friday. Week 1 is then Wednesday to Friday, 36 hours, and week 2 is Monday, Tuesday, Saturday and Sunday, 48 hours. In our example, overtime drops to 8 hours every other week, 32 hours in 8 weeks, with the same 4 of 8 weekends worked.

The 4 on, 4 off repeats every 8 days, so it moves through the week. Some Monday-to-Sunday weeks hold four shifts, 48 hours, and others hold three, 36 hours. In 8 weeks the nurse works 28 shifts (56 days ÷ 8 × 4), which means 4 weeks of four shifts and 4 weeks of three. The four-shift weeks carry 8 hours of overtime each, so 32 hours in total (4 × 8), whichever day the pattern starts.

Some hospitals use the 8-and-80 method instead, which owes overtime after 8 hours in a day and after 80 hours in a 14-day period. The overtime figures above then change. Our [12-hour nurse schedule guide](/blog/12-hour-hospital-shifts-scheduling) shows what that method does to the 2-2-3.

## How Do Weekends Rotate on a Nurse Shift Schedule?

Weekend rotation gives each nurse a set share of weekends, for example every other weekend, working both Saturday and Sunday. On 12-hour shifts that share fits inside a normal 36-hour week. On 8-hour shifts at 40 hours every week, it only fits with a long run of days in a row.

A 12-hour nurse on every other weekend can work Thursday, Saturday and Sunday one week, then Tuesday, Thursday and Friday the next. That is 36 hours each week, no overtime, and never more than 2 days in a row.

An 8-hour nurse at 40 hours every week works five shifts every week. In the week with the weekend off, all five have to fall on Monday to Friday. The weekend just before that week is a working weekend, so Saturday, Sunday and then Monday to Friday make 7 days in a row. We checked every possible layout of a 14-day cycle, and none gets the longest run below 7 days.

There are two ways out. One is 9 shifts every two weeks, a 40-hour week and a 32-hour week, as in the table, with no more than 3 days in a row. The other keeps 10 shifts every two weeks but splits them into 6 shifts one week and 4 the next, a 48-hour week and a 32-hour week. That caps the run at 4 days, but it puts 8 hours of overtime into every other week.

Some hospitals cover weekends with a separate program instead, such as the [weekend-only Baylor shift](/blog/what-is-a-baylor-shift) of two 12-hour weekend shifts. Holidays run on their own rotation, which our guide to a [fair holiday rotation for nurses](/blog/fair-holiday-rotation-nurses-cah) sets out.

## What Are On-Call and PRN Shifts on a Nurse Schedule?

An on-call shift is time when a nurse is off site but must come in if called. A PRN shift, also called per diem, is worked by a nurse with no guaranteed hours who picks up shifts as they are offered. Both fill gaps around the main pattern. A PRN nurse working a shift counts as on duty, and an on-call nurse at home does not.

That last point matters for the staffing rule above. On-call time cannot stand in for the nurse that 42 CFR 485.631(a)(5) requires on duty whenever the hospital has an inpatient.

On-call also sits outside Texas's overtime ban. [Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 258](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/health-and-safety-code/title-4/subtitle-b/chapter-258/) bars a hospital from requiring a nurse to work extra hours or extra days beyond her schedule, and a nurse may refuse. Prescheduled on-call time is excluded from that ban, so on-call written into the schedule is not mandatory overtime under the chapter.

PRN nurses work around the pattern rather than inside it. They take the shifts your regular nurses cannot, so their availability sets how big a gap the pattern can absorb. Our guide to [per diem nursing](/blog/what-is-per-diem-nursing) explains how the arrangement works and how it is paid.

## How Do You Choose a Shift Pattern for a Small Hospital Roster?

Choose the weekend rule and the full-time hours first. Then test each candidate pattern for its busiest week and its longest run of days in a row. The table shows why. The 2-2-3 and the 4 on, 4 off both average 42 hours, yet their busiest weeks differ by 12 hours (60 against 48) and their overtime over 8 weeks by 48 hours (80 against 32).

On a small roster, each nurse in the pattern carries a large share of the shifts. When one nurse goes on leave, her shifts go to someone else. A pattern that already runs at 40 hours every week has no room left before overtime. A 36-hour pattern has 4 hours a week of room. Texas law bars requiring those extra shifts, so they come from volunteers. Our [state guide to mandatory nurse overtime](/blog/mandatory-nurse-overtime-by-state) covers the rules elsewhere.

Write the pattern for one nurse first, check it against those two numbers, and only then copy it across the roster. How many nurses a pattern needs, and the steps to build and post the schedule from it, are in our [nursing staff scheduling guide](/blog/nursing-staff-scheduling-small-hospital).

## What to Do This Week

1. **Write out one nurse's current pattern for two weeks**, day by day, with the start and end time of each shift.
2. **Add up each payroll week on its own**, starting on the day your payroll week starts. Any week over 40 hours carries overtime under the 40-hour workweek, even when the two-week average is under 40.
3. **Find the longest run of days in a row**, including where the pattern wraps from the end of one cycle into the start of the next.
4. **Count the weekends each nurse works in the next 8 weeks**, and compare the highest count with the lowest.
5. **Ask payroll which overtime method you use**, the 40-hour workweek or 8-and-80, because every overtime figure in this guide assumes the 40-hour workweek.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is a 2-2-3 nurse schedule?**

It is a 12-hour pattern on a 14-day loop: two days on, two off, three on, then two off, two on, three off. It gives every other weekend off. Laid out from a Monday, it alternates a 60-hour week with a 24-hour week, so it averages 42 hours with 20 hours of overtime every other week.

**Q: Can an 8-hour nurse work every other weekend without overtime?**

Yes, at 9 shifts every two weeks: one 40-hour week and one 32-hour week, with no more than 3 days in a row. Keeping her at 40 hours every week forces a run of at least 7 days in a row in every two weeks, because the week with the weekend off has to use all five weekdays.

**Q: Does on-call time count as mandatory overtime in Texas?**

No. Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 258 bars a hospital from requiring a nurse to work extra hours or extra days beyond her schedule, but prescheduled on-call time is excluded from that ban. An on-call nurse at home also does not count as the nurse on duty that federal rules require whenever the hospital has an inpatient.
