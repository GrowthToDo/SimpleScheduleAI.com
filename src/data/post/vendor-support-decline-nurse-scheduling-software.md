## Key Takeaways

- Before you sign, ask who your support contact is after go-live and how to reach them on a weekend.
- Public reviews of scheduling software support are mixed, so read recent reviews of each vendor you are considering.
- At a small hospital, the nurse manager may have no one else to hand a scheduling problem to, so a slow answer lands on one person.
- Put support in the contract: a named contact, response times by how urgent the problem is, weekend coverage, and your right to export your data.
- An acquisition is a separate risk. Our guide on [what happens when a scheduling vendor is acquired](/blog/scheduling-software-vendor-acquisition-support) covers it.

During a software demo and setup, you may have a named person who answers quickly. A few months after go-live, that can turn into a support form. Whether that change happens, and how much it matters, depends on the vendor and on what your contract says.

This guide covers what can change, what reviewers of [nurse scheduling software](/nurse-scheduling-software) actually report, and what to write into a contract before you sign. It is aimed at a [critical access hospital](/critical-access-hospital-scheduling) where one nurse manager runs the schedule.

## Why Can Scheduling Software Support Change After Go-Live?

Scheduling software support can change after go-live because setup and ongoing support can be handled by different people. An implementation specialist helps you configure the system, then may move on to the next new customer. After that, questions may go to a general support team that does not know your hospital, your units or your rules.

That handoff is not always a problem. A good support team with good documentation can answer routine questions quickly. It becomes a problem when the answer needs someone who knows how your account was set up, and nobody left on the account does.

Size matters too. A large health system has IT staff and schedulers who can work around a slow answer. A small hospital may have only the nurse manager, who might also be covering shifts. When the schedule will not post on a Friday evening, a support reply on Monday leaves one person to solve it alone. Our guide to [what happens when scheduling software goes down](/blog/nurse-scheduling-software-reliability-crashes) covers how to keep shifts covered while you wait.

## Does an Acquisition Change Scheduling Software Support?

An acquisition can change support, but not always, and reviews alone do not prove it either way. When one company buys another, support teams can be merged or moved. The risk you can check is whether the new owner changes what the product is marketed for.

Aladtec is the example we could verify. [TCP Software acquired Aladtec in October 2021](https://tcpsoftware.com/blog/tcp-acquires-aladtec/). Today, [Aladtec's product page](https://tcpsoftware.com/products/aladtec/) describes it as public safety scheduling software, with no healthcare claim. That is a change in marketing, not a proven drop in support. Our guide on [what happens when your scheduling vendor is acquired](/blog/scheduling-software-vendor-acquisition-support) covers the warning signs and what to ask.

## What Should a Scheduling Software Contract Say About Support?

A scheduling software contract should name who supports you after go-live, how fast they respond to each kind of problem, and when they are available. It should also say what happens if they miss those times, and what happens to your data when the contract ends. If these are only in the sales conversation, they are not guaranteed. Ask for them in writing, alongside the rest of your [software evaluation](/blog/how-to-choose-nurse-scheduling-software).

Start by defining an urgent problem in the contract. An urgent problem is anything that stops shifts being covered. Examples are a schedule that cannot be posted, a system that is down during a scheduling cycle, or an error that leaves a shift uncovered. Ask the vendor to list these as urgent in writing, so a Friday evening problem is not treated like a routine question.

## What Should You Do if Your Scheduling Software Support Has Already Declined?

Write down each problem as it happens: the date, what broke, when you reported it and when it was fixed. After a few weeks, you have a record instead of a feeling. Take that record to your account manager, and ask what the contract says about response times.

If the contract has support terms and the vendor is missing them, the record gives you grounds to escalate. Whether you can also leave early depends on the contract's termination clause, so read it now. When you renew or sign a new contract, ask for a clause that lets you end it if written support terms are missed.

If the contract has no support terms, the record still helps. It turns "support is slow" into specific dates the vendor has to answer.

If you think a switch is coming, do two things first. Export a copy of your schedule history and staff list, so a new system does not start from nothing. Then check how much notice the termination clause asks for, so you know your real leaving date.

## What to Do This Week

1. **Find your current support terms.** Pull your scheduling software contract and look for response times, hours, a named contact and the termination clause.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does scheduling software support always get worse after the contract is signed?**

No. Support varies by vendor, and reviews of the same vendor can disagree. Plan for a change anyway, because a demo cannot show you the support you will get a year later.

**Q: What should count as an urgent support problem for a nurse schedule?**

Anything that stops shifts being covered. The definition just above the contract table lists three examples to name as urgent in writing.

**Q: Can I leave my scheduling software contract if support gets worse?**

It depends on the contract's termination clause, so read it before you need it. When you sign or renew, ask for the right to end the contract early, without a penalty, if written response times are missed.

**Q: Is an acquisition a sign that support will decline?**

Not by itself. An acquisition can bring changes to support teams or to what the product is marketed for, but reviews alone do not prove a support decline. Ask the new owner who supports your account now, and watch whether the product's own pages still describe your kind of hospital.
