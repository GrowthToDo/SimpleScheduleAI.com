## Key Takeaways

- **A swing bed is a change in payment status.** A hospital bed "swings" from acute care to skilled nursing care. The patient does not have to move to a different room or unit.
- **Critical Access Hospitals and rural hospitals under 100 beds can run swing beds.** Medicare covers a swing-bed stay only after a hospital stay of at least three days. Coverage runs up to 100 days in a benefit period.
- **Swing-bed patients stay hospital patients.** The same RN duty applies to them under [42 CFR 485.635(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/485.635), and they share the hospital's 25 beds.
- **The nursing home staffing standard does not carry over.** The swing-bed rule lists eight nursing home requirements that do apply, such as a comprehensive assessment, a team care plan and discharge planning.
- **Texas adds a 30-day rule for Medicaid swing beds.** If its swing beds are used for "more than one 30-day length of stay per year, per resident," a hospital in that program must comply with the full Nursing Facility Requirements. Confirm with HHSC how that is counted.

A swing bed is a hospital bed that can be used for acute care one day and skilled nursing care the next, without the patient changing beds. Patient guides explain what a swing bed means for the family. This guide is for the Director of Nursing and the administrator who have to staff it. It explains the rules and is not legal or billing advice.

## What Is a Swing Bed?

A swing bed is a hospital bed whose payment status changes from acute care to skilled nursing care. The [CMS survey manual for Critical Access Hospitals](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/downloads/som107ap_w_cah.pdf) says: "A 'swing-bed' is a change in reimbursement status." The patient swings from acute-care services to skilled nursing services, and the bed stays where it is. [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/swing-bed-services) describes the hospital providing "either acute hospital or SNF-level care, as needed." SNF stands for skilled nursing facility.

The patient does not have to move. The same manual says "The patient need not change locations in the facility merely because his/her status changes unless the facility requires it." It adds that "Swing-beds need not be located in a special section of the CAH."

For example, a patient admitted for pneumonia may be stable but too weak to go home. Without a swing bed, that patient might transfer to a nursing home, which may be in another town. With one, the patient can stay in the same bed at the local hospital. The hospital changes the patient's status to swing bed, and therapy continues there, close to home.

## Which Hospitals Can Have Swing Beds?

Two kinds of hospitals can run swing beds, under two different rules.

- **Critical Access Hospitals** are covered by [42 CFR 485.645](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/485.645). The hospital must be certified as a Critical Access Hospital and provide "not more than 25 inpatient beds."
- **Other rural hospitals** are covered by [42 CFR 482.58](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/482.58). That hospital must have "fewer than 100 hospital beds," not counting newborn and intensive care beds, and must be "located in a rural area."

Both need a swing-bed approval from CMS.

Our guide to [what a Critical Access Hospital is](/blog/what-is-a-critical-access-hospital) covers the designation.

## Who Qualifies for a Swing Bed, and How Long Can a Patient Stay?

Medicare covers a swing-bed stay only if the patient first had a hospital stay of at least three days. Under [42 CFR 409.30](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/409.30), the patient must have been an inpatient "for at least 3 consecutive calendar days, not counting the date of discharge." Other coverage conditions apply, and Medicaid and private insurance rules differ, so check with your billing office.

Medicare covers up to 100 days. [42 CFR 409.61](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/409.61) says "Up to 100 days are available in each benefit period." A benefit period is Medicare's way of counting one spell of illness. For the first 20 days, Medicare "pays for all covered services." From day 21 to day 100, the patient owes a daily coinsurance amount.

The CMS survey manual sets no length-of-stay limit for a Critical Access Hospital swing-bed patient. It says "There is no length of stay restriction for any CAH swing-bed patient." The 100 days is the Medicare coverage limit, not a facility rule.

## How Does Medicare Pay for a Swing-Bed Stay?

Medicare pays a Critical Access Hospital 101 percent of its reasonable cost for swing-bed care. Reasonable cost is Medicare's measure of the allowable cost of the care, worked out on the hospital's cost report. Ask your finance team for the figure. The [CMS fact sheet on swing beds](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mln006951-swing-bed-services.pdf) says: "We exempt CAH swing bed services from the SNF PPS and pay them based on 101% of reasonable cost of the services." [42 CFR 413.114](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/413.114) sets the same "101 percent of reasonable cost."

Other rural hospitals are paid differently. CMS pays their swing beds under the skilled nursing facility prospective payment system, a set daily rate.

Two paperwork points also apply to a Critical Access Hospital. It does not have to use the Minimum Data Set (MDS), the standard nursing home assessment form. It also files no Payroll-Based Journal (PBJ) staffing data for swing beds, though a separately certified skilled nursing unit does. Our guide to [CMS PBJ reporting](/blog/cms-pbj-reporting-scheduling-software) explains the split.

## How Is a Swing Bed Different From a Nursing Home Bed?

A swing-bed patient remains a hospital patient. The CMS survey manual says "swing-bed patients are not SNF patients. Swing-bed patients in CAHs are considered to be patients of the CAH."

Only some nursing home rules carry over. Section 485.645(d) lists eight requirements, drawn from selected parts of the nursing home regulations, that a Critical Access Hospital must meet for swing-bed patients:

1. Resident rights
2. Admission, transfer and discharge rights
3. Freedom from abuse, neglect and exploitation
4. Social services
5. Comprehensive assessment, comprehensive care plan and discharge planning
6. Specialized rehabilitative services
7. Dental services
8. Nutrition

The nursing home staffing standard, [42 CFR 483.35](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/483.35), is not on that list. So the federal rules do not bring the nursing home staffing numbers onto your floor for swing-bed patients. Our guide to [CNA ratio laws by state](/blog/cna-patient-ratio-laws-by-state) covers what that means for aide staffing.

A separately certified skilled nursing unit is a different case. That unit is certified as a skilled nursing facility and surveyed against the nursing home rules in its own right.

## Do Swing Beds Count Against the 25-Bed Limit?

Yes. Swing beds come out of the same 25 beds. [42 CFR 485.620(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/485.620) says "Inpatient beds may be used for either inpatient or swing-bed services."

A Critical Access Hospital must keep its acute patients' stays to an annual average of 96 hours or less. Swing-bed days do not count toward that average. The CMS fact sheet says Critical Access Hospitals "don't include time spent providing skilled nursing swing bed services" in that calculation. Our guide to [Critical Access Hospital requirements](/blog/critical-access-hospital-requirements) covers the 96-hour rule in full.

## What Nursing Rules Apply to Swing-Bed Patients?

Three federal nursing rules that cover any inpatient also cover a swing-bed patient. The eight nursing home requirements above come on top of them.

- **A licensed nurse on duty whenever there is an inpatient.** [42 CFR 485.631(a)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/485.631) requires a registered nurse, clinical nurse specialist or licensed practical nurse on duty "whenever the CAH has one or more inpatients." The CMS survey manual adds "including patients in a swing bed receiving long term care services."
- **An RN is responsible for each patient's care.** That RN may give the care or assign it to another staff member. 42 CFR 485.635(d)(1) says a registered nurse must provide or assign "the nursing care of each patient, including patients at a SNF level of care in a swing-bed CAH."
- **A care plan for each inpatient.** 485.635(d)(4) says "A nursing care plan must be developed and kept current for each inpatient."

As of November 2026, no federal rule sets a nurse-to-patient ratio for swing-bed patients. The Texas Medicaid rule below may change this for some hospitals; ask HHSC. Our guide to [nurse staffing models](/blog/nurse-staffing-models) explains how the provide-or-assign duty works on a small floor.

## What Extra Work Does a Swing-Bed Patient Add for Nurses?

The eight carried-over requirements add specific tasks for a swing-bed patient. The regulation sets the tasks, and your team decides who does each one.

- **A comprehensive assessment.** A Critical Access Hospital is "not required to use the resident assessment instrument," the MDS form. The assessment itself is still required. The survey manual says "There is no requirement for a CAH to use the MDS form for recording the patient assessment or for nursing care planning," so your own form can record it.
- **A care plan built by a team.** Under [42 CFR 483.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/483.21), the care plan is "Prepared by an interdisciplinary team," meaning staff from more than one discipline. The team includes "A registered nurse with responsibility for the resident" and "A nurse aide with responsibility for the resident."
- **Discharge planning and social services.** Discharge planning and social services are both on the 485.645(d) list. Our advice is to name who owns each one on the day the patient swings.
- **Rehabilitation, dental and nutrition items.** Therapy may come from an outside provider. The nutrition item covers acceptable weight and enough fluid intake.

A swing-bed stay can last weeks. The nurses who knew the patient on the first acute day can still be caring for that patient in week three.

## How Do You Staff Swing Beds When the Census Changes?

Staff the floor to every occupied bed, acute and swing. No federal ratio exists, so the numbers come from your written staffing plan. The plan sets how many nurses each patient count needs.

Here is an illustrative night shift. It is a made-up example, not a real hospital or a customer result. Suppose the staffing plan says 2 licensed nurses and 1 aide for 6 to 8 patients, and 3 licensed nurses and 1 aide for 9 to 12. The floor has 7 acute patients and 3 swing-bed patients. The patient count is 10, so the 9-to-12 row applies: 3 licensed nurses and 1 aide. Counting only the 7 acute patients would have put the night in the smaller row.

The number of swing-bed patients moves with discharges home, acute patients switched to swing-bed status, and transfers to a nursing home. When the count drops, our guide to [low census at a small hospital](/blog/low-census-small-hospital) covers which nurses go home. When it climbs, a [float pool](/blog/float-pool-nursing-small-hospitals) of nurses cross-trained for the emergency department, med-surg and swing beds can cover the gap.

## Does Texas Add Any Swing-Bed Rules?

Yes, for hospitals in the Texas Medicaid Swing Bed Program. The Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) runs that program "for rural hospitals located in counties with populations of 100,000 or less." Under [26 TAC 554.2326](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/26-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-554-2326), a hospital whose swing beds are used for "more than one 30-day length of stay per year, per resident" must "comply with the full Nursing Facility Requirements."

The same rule sets nursing facility payment rates for the program, and adds administrator and staff-development conditions past that threshold. The rule applies only to hospitals in the Texas Medicaid Swing Bed Program. Ask HHSC how it counts the 30-day stays and which nursing facility requirements then apply to your beds.

[26 TAC 505.41](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/26-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-505-41) sets no numeric nurse ratio. As of November 2026, it requires "adequate numbers of RNs, licensed vocational nurses (LVNs), and other personnel." Texas calls a licensed practical nurse a licensed vocational nurse (LVN). The rule also requires an RN on duty in each building where patients are present.

Our list of [critical access hospitals in Texas](/blog/critical-access-hospitals-in-texas) names the hospitals. It does not say which run swing beds or which counties are under 100,000.

## What to Do This Week

1. **Check the census you staff to.** Confirm your written staffing plan counts swing-bed patients along with acute patients.
2. **Name an owner for each swing-bed task.** Write down who does the assessment, the care plan meeting, social services and discharge planning.
3. **Read the eight requirements with your quality lead.** Section 485.645(d) lists them, and the CMS survey manual gives CMS's guidance on each.
4. **Check the Texas 30-day rule.** If you are in the Texas Medicaid Swing Bed Program, ask HHSC how it counts "more than one 30-day length of stay per year, per resident." Then check your swing-bed stays from the last 12 months against that count.
5. **Tell your scheduler when the number of swing-bed patients changes.** Agree on who sends the message and by when.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### The basics

**Q: What is a swing bed in a hospital?**

A swing bed is a hospital bed whose payment status changes from acute care to skilled nursing care. The patient does not have to change rooms. Critical Access Hospitals and rural hospitals under 100 beds can use them.

**Q: How long can a patient stay in a swing bed?**

For a Critical Access Hospital, the CMS survey manual sets no length-of-stay limit. Medicare covers up to 100 days in a benefit period, after a hospital stay of at least three days. The patient owes a daily coinsurance from day 21.

**Q: Is a swing bed the same as a nursing home bed?**

No. A swing-bed patient stays a hospital patient. Eight nursing home requirements apply, such as resident rights and a comprehensive care plan, but the federal nursing home staffing standard does not. In Texas, a hospital in the Medicaid Swing Bed Program must comply with the full Nursing Facility Requirements past "more than one 30-day length of stay per year, per resident"; confirm with HHSC.

### Staffing and rules

**Q: Is there a nurse-to-patient ratio for swing-bed patients?**

As of November 2026, no federal ratio exists. A registered nurse, clinical nurse specialist or licensed practical nurse must be on duty whenever there are inpatients, and a registered nurse must provide or assign each patient's care, including swing-bed patients.

**Q: Do Critical Access Hospitals complete the MDS for swing-bed patients?**

No. A Critical Access Hospital is not required to use the MDS form. It still has to complete a comprehensive assessment and care plan for each swing-bed patient.

**Q: Do swing beds count toward the 25-bed limit?**

Yes. Swing beds come from the same 25 inpatient beds. Swing-bed days are left out of the 96-hour average length of stay.
