## Key Takeaways

- On October 23, 2026, [the state's Rural Texas Strong grant list](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/rural-health-transformation-program) showed **$223.0 million** approved for rural providers for the first year, across 441 applications.
- **$87.4 million of that $223.0 million, on 159 applications, is under a contract signed by both sides.**
- The state agency running the program says a grant "is not considered awarded until a contract has been fully executed", which means signed by both sides. Unsigned money is not an award yet.
- 86 of the 92 Texas Critical Access Hospitals on the [Flex Monitoring Team's list](https://www.flexmonitoring.org/critical-access-hospital-locations) appear on the state's list. 62 of those 86 hospitals have at least one signed contract.
- The "$8 million" [CMS announced on October 22](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/trump-administration-announces-8-million-28-rural-healthcare-providers-strengthen-healthcare-across) is the 28 applications the state added on October 14. By October 23, one of those 28 applications had a signed contract.

Most of the rural health grant money Texas has approved is still waiting for a signature. Texas runs the federal Rural Health Transformation Program as "Rural Texas Strong", through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC). HHSC posts a list of approved grant applications and updates it every week. We read the October 8 and October 23 lists line by line, and matched each public announcement to the list or to the state's budget.

## What Does an Approved but Unsigned Rural Texas Strong Grant Mean?

An approved but unsigned grant means HHSC has finished negotiating with the provider and plans to send a contract, but the contract is not yet signed. HHSC calls the approved but unsigned stage "Intent to Award". HHSC explains "Intent to Award" at the top of [the October 8 list](https://web.archive.org/web/20261015203056/https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/rural-hlth-prgm/2026/10-8-2026-list-award-recipients.xlsx):

> "A grant is not considered awarded until a contract has been fully executed. "Intent to Award" is the status during the pendency of the contract execution process after HHSC has completed negotiations with the applicant and has determined that HHSC will proceed with issuing a contract agreement to award the grant."

"Fully executed" means signed by both sides. Every row on the list is approved. A column headed "Awarded (Contract Fully Executed)" says Yes once the contract is signed.

Each row is one application, so a hospital can appear more than once, once for each grant the hospital applied for. Each grant shows a total over several years and a first-year part. HHSC labels the first-year part "Year 1 subset" and does not define "Year 1 subset" further. Every dollar figure in this piece is the first-year part.

[The Governor's October 22 release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/951727832/governor-abbott-announces-8-million-in-rural-texas-strong-grants) says the same: "Funding will be available after contracts are finalized."

## How Much Rural Texas Strong Grant Money Is Signed?

$87.4 million of the $223.0 million on [HHSC's October 23 list](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/rural-hlth-prgm/10-23-2026-list-award-recipients.xlsx). The $87.4 million of signed money sits on 159 of the 441 applications. The grants come out of the $281.3 million Texas received from the federal government for the first year, [the Governor's office says](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/951727832/governor-abbott-announces-8-million-in-rural-texas-strong-grants). This piece calls the $281.3 million Texas's first-year federal award.

Two weeks earlier, $51 million was signed out of $129 million on the list. Signing grew, but so did the list.

[HHSC's program page](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/rural-health-transformation-program) names six initiatives, or areas of spending. The list has grants under three of the six initiatives. Most of the signed money is in one kind of grant: the prevention grants to hospital districts.

| Kind of grant on HHSC's October 23 list       | Money on the list, and how much of that money is signed |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Prevention grants to rural hospital districts | $54.0 million, of which $51.75 million signed           |
| Prevention grants open to all rural hospitals | $68.0 million, of which $15.7 million signed            |
| Workforce grants, first round                 | $29.1 million, of which $10.3 million signed            |
| Workforce grants, second round                | $3.7 million, none signed                               |
| Equipment and facility grants                 | $68.2 million, of which $9.7 million signed             |
| All grants on the list                        | $223.0 million, of which $87.4 million signed           |

## How Many Texas Critical Access Hospitals Are on the Rural Texas Strong List?

86 of the 92 hospitals on the Flex Monitoring Team's Texas list. The Flex Monitoring Team publishes the named list of Critical Access Hospitals in each state; we used the Texas rows retrieved September 4, 2026. Our [guide to Texas Critical Access Hospitals](/blog/critical-access-hospitals-in-texas) prints all 92.

Those 86 hospitals have applications worth $132.3 million of the $223.0 million on the list. 62 of the 86 hospitals have at least one signed contract.

Many of the 86 hospitals do not apply under the hospital's own name. A hospital district, a hospital authority, a county or an operating company applies on the hospital's behalf. So a hospital can be on the list under a name the hospital's staff would not search for first.

## Does Any Rural Texas Strong Grant Pay for Nurses?

Some grants fund workforce recruiting and retention. 66 Critical Access Hospitals hold a workforce-grant application, and 24 of those 66 have a signed workforce-grant contract. [HHSC's program page](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/rural-health-transformation-program) lists four uses for workforce grants: career paths for local high school students, scholarships for recent graduates, relocation or signing bonuses, and new residency or fellowship programs.

The list itself does not say what any grant pays for. The list gives names, amounts and contract status. So no one can tell from the list that a hospital received money for nurses. One line in [HHSC's approved first-year budget](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/rural-hlth-prgm/aprvd-bdgt-prd-1-rvsd-bdgt-narr.pdf) is about nursing, an agreement with the Texas Board of Nursing. The budget says the Board of Nursing agreement "remains budgeted at $0.00 in Budget Period 1 and is projected to begin in Year 2."

In plain words, nothing is planned for the Board of Nursing agreement in the first year.

Whether federal rules let Rural Texas Strong money pay a nurse's salary is a separate question. We answered the salary question in [Can Rural Health Transformation Money Pay Nurse Salaries?](/articles/rural-health-transformation-deadline-nurse-salaries)

## Do the Rural Texas Strong Announcements Match the State's Grant List?

Two of the announcements match the list exactly. Both announcements are sums of named grants.

| Announcement                                                                                                                                                         | Amount announced                                       | What it is on HHSC's list                                                       | Signed by October 23     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| [The Governor, September 28, 2026, as reported by FOX 7 Austin](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/gov-greg-abbott-announces-51m-grant-program-rural-texas-health-care) | $51 million, $750,000 to each of 68 hospital districts | The 68 hospital-district prevention grants already signed on the October 8 list | All 68 grants            |
| [The Governor](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/951727832/governor-abbott-announces-8-million-in-rural-texas-strong-grants) and CMS, October 22, 2026            | $8,145,444.89 (the Governor), "$8 million" (CMS)       | The 28 applications HHSC added to the list on October 14                        | 1 of the 28 applications |

The September grants are 68 × $750,000 = $51,000,000. HHSC's budget planned hospital-district prevention grants at "approximately $750,000 per award, to rural hospital districts".

The October 14 batch adds up to exactly $8,145,444.89, the Governor's figure. The Governor's release says HHSC "plans to award" the batch and calls the batch "a portion of" Texas's first-year federal award. [The CMS release](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/trump-administration-announces-8-million-28-rural-healthcare-providers-strengthen-healthcare-across) names two providers, Sudan Medical Clinic and Best Value Medical Center Pharmacy, and mentions funding for Marble Falls. All three providers are in the October 14 batch.

The earlier [$60 million](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-hhsc-announce-60-million-in-federal-funding-to-make-rural-texans-healthy-again), [$99 million](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-hhsc-announce-99-million-in-federal-funding-for-rural-hospitals-to-recruit-workers) and two $56 million announcements (on [May 1](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-hhsc-announce-56-million-in-federal-funding-for-rural-health-care-providers-to-modernize-equipment-infrastructure) and [May 5](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-hhsc-announce-56-million-in-federal-funding-for-rural-prevention-and-wellness-programs)) were amounts the state planned to spend, not grants to named providers.

## Does Unsigned Rural Texas Strong Money Have to Be Committed by October 30?

The federal deadline is clear. Texas must commit the money from Texas's first budget year, which ends October 30, 2026. To commit money means to promise the money to a named party, for example in a signed contract. The federal rules call committing money "obligating" funds.

[CMS's April 2026 questions and answers](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/frequently-asked-questions-april-2026.pdf) are direct about money a state does not commit in time:

> "Therefore, failing to obligate funds within the allowed timeframe will result in those funds being returned and redistributed."

CMS also says states cannot get more time to spend the funds. HHSC has not said whether approved grants without a signed contract count as committed. Our [article on the October 30 deadline](/articles/rural-health-transformation-deadline-nurse-salaries) covers the federal calendar in full.

## What to Do This Week

1. **Find your hospital on HHSC's list.** The latest list is linked from [HHSC's Rural Health Transformation page](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/rural-health-transformation-program). Search for your hospital's legal name, your hospital district or authority name, and your county. Your hospital can appear more than once, once for each grant.
2. **Read the "Awarded (Contract Fully Executed)" column on each of your rows.** Yes means the contract is signed by both sides. No means the grant is approved but not yet awarded.
3. **If a row says No, ask whoever signs contracts for your hospital where the unsigned contract stands.** HHSC describes the Intent to Award stage as the time when the contract is being executed. The next step may be a signature on your side.
4. **Do not plan or hire against unsigned money.** The Governor's office says funding will be available after contracts are finalized.
5. **Check the list each week.** HHSC says the list is updated weekly.
6. **If a signed grant will pay for people, read the federal salary rules first.** Our [article on Rural Health Transformation money and nurse salaries](/articles/rural-health-transformation-deadline-nurse-salaries) explains the three conditions.

**Q: What does "Intent to Award" mean for a Rural Texas Strong grant?**

HHSC has approved the application and plans to send a contract, but the contract is not yet signed by both sides. HHSC says a grant "is not considered awarded until a contract has been fully executed."

**Q: How much Rural Texas Strong grant money is under signed contract?**

$87.4 million of the $223.0 million on HHSC's October 23, 2026 list. The $87.4 million and the $223.0 million are the first-year part of each grant. The signed money sits on 159 of the 441 applications.

**Q: Was the $8 million announced on October 22 new money?**
