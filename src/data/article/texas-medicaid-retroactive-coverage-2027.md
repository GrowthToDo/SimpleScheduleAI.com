## Key Takeaways

- **Texas Medicaid will pay for less of the time before an application.** That payment is called retroactive coverage: Medicaid paying for care a patient got before applying. Until December 31, 2026, it reaches back three months before the month of application. For applications made on or after January 1, 2027, it reaches back two months, under [section 71112 of the 2025 federal budget law](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm).
- **The count starts from the month the application is filed.** How long Texas takes to decide it does not change which months are covered.
- **Some 2026 stays need an application soon.** An August 2026 stay is covered only if the application is filed by November 30. September and October 2026 stays need it filed by December 31.
- **Texas is slow to decide applications right now.** In late August, Texas decided 62% of Medicaid applications within the 45-day federal standard, down from more than 90%, [according to state data reported by KERA](https://www.tpr.org/news/2026-09-01/texas-medicaid-application-backlog-grows-to-more-than-210-000-state-data-shows).
- **Our view is that the fix is timing.** Helping uninsured inpatients apply before they go home keeps their stay inside the covered months under either rule.

From January 1, 2027, Texas Medicaid will pay for care a patient received up to two months before the month they apply, down from three. Because the window is one month shorter, an uninsured patient who waits to apply has less time before part of a hospital stay stops being covered. The months that matter most right now are August, September and October 2026.

## What Changes in Texas Medicaid Retroactive Coverage on January 1, 2027?

The retroactive window is how far back Medicaid will pay for care a patient got before applying, as long as the patient would have qualified at the time. Federal law has let it reach back to "the third month before the month in which the recipient makes application." The Texas rule for pregnant women's Medicaid, [1 TAC 366.325](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/1-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-366-325), says: "Retroactive coverage may begin as early as three months before the application month."

The 2025 federal budget law, P.L. 119-21, changes that in section 71112. For a state that has not expanded Medicaid, such as Texas, coverage now reaches back to "the second month before the month in which the individual made application." The change applies to applications made on or after January 1, 2027.

In plain terms, the covered months are the month of the application plus the two months before it. Today they are the month of the application plus the three months before it.

The deadlines that matter now come straight from the table. An August 2026 stay needs an application filed by November 30. A September or October 2026 stay needs one filed by December 31. A November 2026 stay is still covered by a January 2027 application, because January plus the two months before it reaches November.

## Does the Texas Medicaid Backlog Shorten the Retroactive Window?

No. The law counts back from the month "in which the individual made application," which is the month the application is filed. A decision that takes longer does not move that starting point.

What the backlog does is make hospitals wait longer for a decision. Texas had about 1,600 pending Medicaid applications in January 2026 and more than 210,000 in August. Those figures come from state data released by Texans Care for Children, a statewide research and advocacy group, and [reported by KERA](https://www.tpr.org/news/2026-09-01/texas-medicaid-application-backlog-grows-to-more-than-210-000-state-data-shows). KERA describes 45 days as the federal standard processing time. In late August Texas decided 62% of applications within 45 days, so about 4 in 10 took longer.

Our reading is that a hospital cannot bill Medicaid for a patient until the patient is approved. So the backlog costs your hospital time, while a late application can cost the stay itself.

## Who in Texas Does the Shorter Medicaid Window Apply To?

Every Texas Medicaid applicant from January 1, 2027. The new law gives one month to adults covered through a Medicaid expansion and two months to everyone else. Texas has not expanded Medicaid, so it has no expansion group. Children, pregnant women and the other groups Texas covers today all move to two months.

Our earlier article on [when a Texas Medicaid expansion could start](/articles/texas-medicaid-expansion-earliest-date) covers the expansion side. If Texas did expand, adults in that new group would get the one-month window.

## What Is Texas Doing About the Medicaid Application Backlog?

The Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) has asked the Legislature for money to make 642 temporary eligibility workers permanent. Eligibility workers are the state staff who decide applications. The request was [reported by KERA](https://www.tpr.org/public-health/2026-09-11/texas-health-agency-asks-lawmakers-for-additional-funding-to-address-growing-medicaid-snap-backlogs). Diana Forester, director of health policy at Texans Care for Children, told KERA those positions are set to expire at the end of the current state fiscal year. The Legislature meets in January 2027, so any new money would come through that session.

## What to Do This Week

1. **Find uninsured patients from August 2026 who have not applied.** An August stay is covered only if the application is filed by November 30. Ask your business office for the names this week.
2. **Then do the same for September and October 2026.** Those applications must be filed by December 31. Your staff can contact these patients and help them apply.
3. **Move application help before discharge.** Ask whoever helps patients apply for Medicaid, often patient access or a financial counselor, to start the application during the stay.
4. **Record the filing date on each account.** From January, the filing month decides which months are covered. Keep it where billing staff can see it.
5. **Tell your admitting and emergency staff about January 1.** A short note is enough: from January, an uninsured patient's application should go in during the stay, or at the latest in the same month.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is Texas Medicaid retroactive coverage?**

It is Medicaid paying for care a patient received before applying, as long as the patient would have qualified at the time. Until December 31, 2026, it covers the month of application plus the three months before it. From January 1, 2027, it covers the month of application plus the two months before it.

**Q: Does a slow Medicaid decision shorten the retroactive window?**

No. The window counts back from the month the application is filed, so a slow decision does not change which months are covered.

**Q: Does the January 2027 change apply to children and pregnant women in Texas?**

Yes. The two-month rule applies to everyone except adults covered through a Medicaid expansion, and Texas has not expanded Medicaid.

**Q: Which 2026 hospital stays need an application before the change?**

An August 2026 stay needs an application filed by November 30. A September or October 2026 stay needs one filed by December 31. A November 2026 stay is still covered if the application is filed in January 2027; after that, the two-month window has moved past November.
