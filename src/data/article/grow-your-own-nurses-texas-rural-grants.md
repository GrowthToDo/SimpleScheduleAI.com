## Key Takeaways

- **By our reading, yes, through a scholarship for a recent high school graduate.** The [Round 1 rules for Texas's rural workforce grants](https://web.archive.org/web/20260516085149/https://grants.hhs.texas.gov/vforcesite/sfc/servlet.shepherd/version/download/068cr00000nMsosAAC) allow scholarships for "recent high school graduates". The scholarship must be for a healthcare program the hospital needs. A nursing program fits that wording, though the state has not said so directly. Each scholarship holder must agree to work at the hospital for at least five years.
- **The rules do not mention sending current employees to nursing school.** The rules allow four ways to spend the money. None of the four lists sending an aide already on staff to nursing school, unless the aide is a recent high school graduate. Federal guidance gives a wider example: [CMS lists tuition reimbursement](https://www.medicaid.gov/resources-for-states/rural-health/rhtp-resources/CatE-Workforce-Info-Guide.pdf) for people studying to become a licensed practical nurse.
- **A $725,000 grant's first year could pay nursing school for up to 16 or 17 students.** The largest workforce grant is $725,000, paid out over the years to April 2031. The rules cap the first year, which ends September 30, 2027, at $221,995 of a $725,000 grant. At [Cisco College's 2026-27 cost](https://www.cisco.edu/uploads/files/general/VN-Cost-Sheet-2026-2027.pdf) for its vocational nursing program, $221,995 covers 16 or 17 students. The figure is our illustration, not a grant anyone received.
- **Six Panhandle hospitals train their own nurses a different way.** The hospitals pay the nursing instructors' salaries and the students pay tuition, [according to HHSC](https://web.archive.org/web/20260423211338/https://www.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/rural-stars-nurse-education.pdf).
- **Only hospitals already on the state's list can use this money now.** Both 2026 application rounds have closed. 66 of Texas's 92 Critical Access Hospitals have a workforce-grant application on the state's October 23 list, and 24 of those 66 have a signed contract.

A small Texas hospital with a state rural workforce grant can, by our reading of the rules, pay for a recent high school graduate's nursing school. The graduate has to sign on to work at the hospital for five years. The rules do not mention paying for a current employee to become a nurse. We read the state's application rules, the federal guidance behind them and the state's October 23 grant list to find out what a hospital can pay for.

## What Does It Mean for a Rural Hospital to Grow Its Own Nurses?

Growing your own means training nurses from your own town instead of recruiting nurses from somewhere else. On October 26, the [Daily Yonder](https://dailyyonder.com/looking-local-could-be-a-solution-to-rural-nursing-shortage/2026/10/26/), a news site that covers rural America, described two ways rural hospitals do it. One way starts with local students. The other way sends the hospital's own employees through nursing school while the employees keep working.

Chrissy Curcio is chief nursing officer at A.O. Fox Hospital in Oneonta, New York. Curcio was speaking about the supply of new nursing students. She told the Daily Yonder: "There's just not the same funnel there was in the past." Ryan Gates of Western Governors University described a California hospital that sent employees to nursing school online. Those employees kept their jobs, "staying gainfully employed and providing for their families."

The shortage is larger outside cities. The federal Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) [projects](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/nursing-projections-factsheet.pdf) that nonmetro areas, the counties outside metropolitan areas, will have 24% fewer registered nurses than projected demand in 2028. For metro areas, HRSA projects 5% fewer.

## Can Rural Texas Strong Grants Pay for Nursing School?

By our reading, yes, for a recent high school graduate. The money comes from the Rural Health Transformation Program, a federal program run by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). Texas runs its share as Rural Texas Strong, through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC). The workforce grants are the part HHSC calls Initiative 4, "The Next Generation of the Small Town Doctor and Team". HHSC took workforce-grant applications in two rounds, which closed on [June 9](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/TXHHSC/bulletins/4186ccb) and [August 26, 2026](https://resources.hhs.texas.gov/rfa/hhs0017618).

[HHSC's rulebook for Round 1 applicants](https://web.archive.org/web/20260516085149/https://grants.hhs.texas.gov/vforcesite/sfc/servlet.shepherd/version/download/068cr00000nMsosAAC), called a Request for Applications, allows four ways to spend a grant. The four ways are career paths for local high school students, scholarships for recent high school graduates, relocation or signing bonuses, and new residency programs. Each hospital chose one or more of the four ways in its application. The scholarship rule reads:

> "providing scholarships to recent high school graduates who will enroll, or to those who are already enrolled in, a healthcare program that would produce a professional that is identified as needed by Grantee."

In plain words, a hospital can give a scholarship to a recent high school graduate who is training for a job the hospital needs filled. A nursing program fits that rule if the hospital names nurses as the job it needs. The rulebook never uses the word "tuition", never names nursing school, and never says how recent "recent" is. Each scholarship holder must agree to work at the hospital "for a minimum of five years through a contract or agreement." We could not read the Round 2 rulebook, so this article describes the Round 1 rules.

The signing bonus rule keeps hospitals from hiring each other's nurses. The rulebook says "Recruitment of professionals already practicing in rural counties in Texas is not an eligible activity." A bonus can go to a nurse first licensed in Texas within the previous 12 months. A bonus can also go to a nurse coming from another state, or from a Texas county of 68,751 people or more, which the rulebook treats as not rural. The hospital also may not make the nurse sign a non-compete agreement.

The student loan row comes from [CMS's program questions and answers](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/rural-health-transformation-frequently-asked-questions.pdf): "Issuing direct student loans and funding student loan repayment programs are unallowable uses of RHT funds."

Current employees are the open question. The Daily Yonder's employee model has no matching rule in the Texas rulebook. The federal guidance gives a wider example. The [CMS workforce guide](https://www.medicaid.gov/resources-for-states/rural-health/rhtp-resources/CatE-Workforce-Info-Guide.pdf) lists "Tuition reimbursements to individuals studying to become a licensed practical nurse (LPN) who commit to serving in rural areas." Texas calls a licensed practical nurse a licensed vocational nurse, or LVN. The CMS example covers any person who commits to rural service, with no limit to recent high school graduates. The Texas rulebook does not include that example.

The Texas rulebook does allow one kind of spending on current staff. Up to 10% of a grant may go to "retention activities specifically designed to support and retain individuals serving in mentorship, preceptorship, or training roles". A nurse who trains students or new hires fits that wording.

## How Many Nursing Students Could One Rural Texas Strong Grant Pay For?

Up to 16 or 17 students in the first year of the largest grant, if the whole first-year share went to vocational nursing scholarships. Here is how the number is built.

1. **The largest grant is $725,000.** The rulebook sets an "Estimated Max Award Amount" of $725,000, and each applicant may apply once.
2. **The grant is paid out in five spending periods.** The rulebook spreads each grant from September 30, 2026 to April 30, 2031. No more than 30.62% may be spent in the first period, which ends September 30, 2027.
3. **The first period's share is $221,995.** $725,000 × 30.62% = $221,995. On HHSC's October 23 list, 67 of the 70 workforce grants of exactly $725,000 show $221,995 as the first-year part. The other three show less, from $199,030 to $221,994.62.
4. **One student's vocational nursing program costs $12,838 to $13,418.** Cisco College is a community college in Cisco, Texas, and its vocational nursing program trains students for the LVN license. The range is [Cisco College's 2026-27 total](https://www.cisco.edu/uploads/files/general/VN-Cost-Sheet-2026-2027.pdf) for a Texas resident living outside the college's taxing district. The total includes the college's estimate of out-of-pocket costs, which varies with optional items. Prerequisite courses are extra.
5. **$221,995 ÷ $13,418 = 16.5, and $221,995 ÷ $12,838 = 17.3.** So the first-year share covers 16 or 17 students at most.

The real number would be lower. A grant also pays for whatever else the hospital applied to do, and the scholarship rule covers recent high school graduates who sign the five-year agreement. The calculation is our illustration, not a grant anyone received.

A hospital without a workforce grant on HHSC's list cannot use this route now, because both 2026 rounds have closed. A hospital that is on the list should also check its contract. HHSC's list says a grant "is not considered awarded until a contract has been fully executed", which means signed by both sides.

## How Does the Texas Panhandle Nursing Consortium Train Its Own Nurses?

The hospitals pay the teachers, and the students pay the tuition. An [HHSC article from February 17, 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260423211338/https://www.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/rural-stars-nurse-education.pdf) describes the Rural Nursing Education Consortium in the Texas Panhandle. Six hospitals, in Dumas, Hereford, Dalhart, Borger, Perryton and Canadian, share the cost of training licensed vocational nurses and registered nurses. The six hospitals work with Amarillo College, Frank Phillips College and West Texas A&M.

Five of the six are Critical Access Hospitals on the [Flex Monitoring Team's Texas list](https://www.flexmonitoring.org/critical-access-hospital-locations), the hospitals in Dumas, Dalhart, Borger, Perryton and Canadian. The Flex Monitoring Team publishes the named list of Critical Access Hospitals in each state. Our [guide to Texas Critical Access Hospitals](/blog/critical-access-hospitals-in-texas) lists all five.

Candace Smith, CEO of the Deaf Smith County Hospital District in Hereford, told HHSC what each hospital pays for. Each hospital pays the salary of an LVN clinical instructor, the salary of an instructor for nurses moving from LVN to RN, and supplies. Jeff Turner, CEO of the Moore County Hospital District in Dumas, said each instructor can teach up to 10 students per curriculum year. Yessenia Longoria, Moore County's chief nursing officer, said the students pay tuition and expect something from that investment.

The payoff was slow. "We didn't see the fruits of our labor for probably three years," Smith told HHSC. HHSC reports that the Hereford hospital has since stopped needing agency, or contract, nurses.

All six hospitals have a workforce-grant application on HHSC's October 23 list, under the hospital's own name or its district's name. Four of the six have a signed contract. The list does not say what any grant pays for, so the list does not show whether any of the six grants funds the consortium.

## Is the Texas $15,000 Rural Nurse Stipend Open in 2026?

We could not confirm that a new round is open. The Texas Department of Agriculture runs the Rural Nursing Recruitment and Retention Program. A July 20, 2026 copy of the [department's program page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260720210637/https://texasagriculture.gov/Grants-Services/RNRR) reads "Deadline: Coming Spring 2026", with no new application posted. We could not load the live page on October 29, 2026.

The [2024 round](https://web.archive.org/web/20240603211852/https://texasagriculture.gov/Portals/0/Publications/ER/SORH/RNRR/2024%20RNRR%20Program_RFGA.pdf) paid "$15,000 to rural Texas health care facilities to provide recruitment and retention stipends to nurses working full-time in their facility." For this program, a rural facility was one in a Texas county of 68,000 people or fewer. The nurse had to work full-time for 36 months in a row, and the state paid the facility only after the 36 months. Applications closed June 27, 2024. The stipend rewards a nurse for staying, so the stipend does not pay for nursing school.

## What to Do This Week

1. **Find your hospital on HHSC's list.** The latest list is linked from [HHSC's Rural Health Transformation page](https://pfd.hhs.texas.gov/rural-health-transformation-program). Search your hospital's legal name, your hospital district and your county. Check whether your workforce-grant row says Yes under "Awarded (Contract Fully Executed)".
2. **Ask whoever wrote your grant application which of the four ways the application chose.** The CEO or the grant writer holds the application. The list shows the amount, not the way the money will be spent.
3. **If your application chose scholarships, ask HHSC in writing whether nursing school counts.** HHSC's program page gives RuralTexasStrong@hhs.texas.gov for Rural Texas Strong questions. Ask in the same email whether a current employee who graduated high school recently would qualify.
4. **Have the five-year agreement written before paying any scholarship.** The rulebook requires the agreement "through a contract or agreement". CMS has suggested that states pay scholarships "on a yearly basis", with money forfeited for years of service not completed. Ask HHSC whether your hospital should pay scholarships year by year.
5. **Price one program at your nearest community college.** Use the college's published cost for a Texas resident in your county, as in the Cisco College example above.
6. **Check the [Department of Agriculture's stipend page](https://texasagriculture.gov/Grants-Services/RNRR) for a new round.** The 2024 round paid $15,000 per facility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can Rural Texas Strong grants pay for nursing school?**

By our reading, yes, as a scholarship for a recent high school graduate in a healthcare program the hospital needs, such as a nursing program. HHSC has not said so directly. The scholarship holder must agree to work at the hospital for at least five years.

**Q: Can a Rural Texas Strong grant pay for a current employee's nursing school?**

The Texas rules do not mention paying for a current employee's nursing school. An employee who is a recent high school graduate fits the scholarship rule. For anyone else, ask HHSC in writing before spending grant money.

**Q: How much is the largest Rural Texas Strong workforce grant?**

$725,000 per applicant, paid out in five spending periods from September 30, 2026 to April 30, 2031. No more than 30.62% may be spent in the first period, so the first-year share of a $725,000 grant is $221,995.

**Q: Can a Texas rural hospital use the workforce grant to repay a nurse's student loans?**

No. CMS says student loan repayment is not an allowed use of Rural Health Transformation money.
