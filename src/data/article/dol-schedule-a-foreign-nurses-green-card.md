## Key Takeaways

- **It is a proposal, not law.** The Labor Department published a [proposed rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23370/modernizing-the-labor-market-test-and-improving-protections-for-us-workers-in-the-perm-immigrant) on November 17, 2026 that would end Schedule A. Schedule A lets a hospital sponsor a foreign-educated nurse for a green card without first advertising the job to U.S. workers. Comments are open until January 19, 2027.
- **Hospitals could still sponsor foreign-educated nurses.** A hospital would first advertise the job for at least 90 days. A Labor Department review would follow, averaging 300 to 360 days as of October 31, 2026.
- **The extra paperwork adds about 14 to 17 months.** The 14 to 17 months is our math: 120 to 150 days of recruiting (at least 90 days, plus 30 to 60 days before filing), plus the department's 300 to 360 days of review.
- **By our reading, the nurse's start date moves back far less.** A nurse's place in the visa line is set when the Labor Department accepts the application, before its review. That place moves back about four to five months. Visa numbers are now going to nurses who joined the line about two and a half years ago, so the review runs while the nurse waits anyway.
- **The proposal would bar some hospitals from the process.** An employer that laid off U.S. workers in the same or a similar job at that location in the past two years could not use it.

The Labor Department has proposed ending Schedule A for nurses. Schedule A lets a hospital sponsor a foreign-educated registered nurse for a green card without first advertising the job to U.S. workers. A law firm partner told Newsweek that each nurse case would add more than a year before the green card petition can be filed, as [reported by Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/labor-department-schedule-foreign-nurses-green-card-proposal-481556). That figure matches our math for the paperwork. By our reading, the nurse's start date moves back much less, about four to five months while visa waits stay this long, and this piece shows the math. It is not legal advice; check any specific case with your immigration attorney.

## What Did the Labor Department Propose for Nurse Green Cards?

The Labor Department proposed ending Schedule A for nurses and physical therapists. The [proposed rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23370/modernizing-the-labor-market-test-and-improving-protections-for-us-workers-in-the-perm-immigrant) lists "eliminating further administration of Schedule A" among its changes. Schedule A has two groups, and "Group I consists of physical therapists and professional nurses."

The department gives two reasons. It says it "no longer possesses evidence that there is a nationwide shortage" of U.S. workers for these jobs. It also says the immigration law does not let it make such a shortage finding. Without Schedule A, a hospital would sponsor a nurse through PERM labor certification, the Labor Department's standard test of whether U.S. workers are available for a job.

## Is the Rule Final, and When Could It Take Effect?

No. It is a proposed rule, published in the Federal Register on November 17, 2026 under docket ETA-2026-0133. The department takes comments until January 19, 2027, and a final rule could differ.

The proposal sets no effective date. It describes "a 60-day delayed effective date plus an additional 30 calendar days" of transition, counted from when a final rule is published. Until a final rule is published and takes effect, Schedule A still applies. The proposal does not clearly say how cases already filed would be treated.

## How Does a Hospital Sponsor a Foreign-Educated Nurse Today?

Today a hospital does not have to advertise the job first. Under [20 CFR 656.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/656.5), the nurse qualifies in one of three ways: a certificate from the Commission on Graduates of Foreign Nursing Schools (CGFNS), a full and unrestricted state nursing license, or a passing score on the NCLEX-RN licensing exam.

The hospital then takes two steps:

1. **It gets a prevailing wage determination.** The Labor Department sets the wage the job must pay.
2. **It files the green card petition directly with the immigration agency.** The petition is Form I-140, and it goes to U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). Under [20 CFR 656.15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/656.15), the application goes to the immigration agency, not to the Labor Department.

The wage step is not new under the proposal. The Federal Register notice says Schedule A employers already get a wage determination from the Labor Department but skip the full labor certification.

## What Would a Hospital Have to Do Instead?

The hospital would run the full PERM process before filing the green card petition. Three changes matter for a small hospital.

1. **Recruiting for at least 90 days.** Job advertisements must run "no less than 90 calendar days," including a job order with the state workforce agency. Recruiting must finish "no more than 60 calendar days and no fewer than 30 calendar days before the expected filing" of the application.
2. **A Labor Department review.** The department's [processing times page](https://flag.dol.gov/processingtimes) showed an average of 300 to 360 calendar days for a PERM review, as of October 31, 2026.
3. **A layoff bar.** The proposal "prohibits employers who have laid off U.S. workers in the same or similar occupation and at the place(s) of employment within the most recent 2 years" from using the process. A hospital that laid off nurses at that site in the past two years should ask its attorney whether the bar would apply.

## How Much Longer Would a Sponsored Nurse Take to Start Work?

The paperwork would take about 14 to 17 months longer, by our math. Recruiting takes at least 90 days, and the application is filed 30 to 60 days after recruiting ends: 120 to 150 days in all. The review adds 300 to 360 days. At the low end, 120 + 300 = 420 days. At the high end, 150 + 360 = 510 days. That is about 14 to 17 months before the hospital can file the green card petition. We assume each step starts after the one before, including recruiting starting after the wage determination is issued, and no audit.

The start date moves back less, because of how the visa line works. A sponsored nurse waits for a visa number in a line ordered by a date called the priority date. Under [8 CFR 204.5(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/204.5), the two paths set that date at different moments:

- **Schedule A today:** the date the green card petition "is properly filed with USCIS."
- **The proposed path:** the date "the labor certification application was accepted for processing" by the Labor Department. The acceptance comes before the 300-to-360-day review starts.

The line is long. In the [November 2026 Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin/2027/visa-bulletin-for-november-2026.html), visas for professionals and skilled workers (the EB-3 category) were going to people whose priority date was June 15, 2024 or earlier, for most countries. So the newest place in line now getting a visa number was set about two years and five months ago. For nurses born in the Philippines, the date was August 15, 2023, about three years and three months back. The Labor Department review takes 10 to 12 months.

So the nurse joins the line about four to five months later than under Schedule A, and the review runs while the nurse waits. The start date then moves back by about the same four to five months, under three conditions:

- **The wait stays long.** It has to be longer than the 10-to-12-month Labor Department review plus the immigration agency's decision on the green card petition.
- **The line keeps moving at roughly its recent pace.** If the line slowed, a later place in it would cost more than four to five months.
- **Nothing goes wrong.** A Labor Department audit or a denial would add time.

If the wait ever got shorter than the review plus the immigration agency's decision, the delay would grow by the difference. With no line at all, the full 14 to 17 months would show up. If recruiting can run during the wage determination wait, the shift would be smaller than four to five months. The four-to-five-month figure is our reading of the rules, so confirm it for any specific nurse with your immigration attorney.

## How Many Texas Nurses Were First Licensed Abroad?

About one Texas registered nurse in eleven. The [Texas Center for Nursing Workforce Studies](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/chs/hprc/publications/Posters/IEN%20Profile%20Poster_48x36.pdf) analyzed 2023 Texas Board of Nursing licensure data. It counted 21,124 internationally educated RNs, "9.1 percent of the Texas RN workforce." The count is based on the country of a nurse's first license. Internationally educated nurses made up "11 percent of the hospital RN workforce," and the Philippines was the most common country of first license.

That count includes nurses who are already U.S. citizens or permanent residents, so it is not a count of nurses waiting for green cards. We found no data on how many small or rural Texas hospitals sponsor nurses, so we cannot say how many hospitals this proposal touches. Our earlier piece on whether rural hospitals face a [nurse shortage or a retention problem](/articles/nurse-shortage-or-retention-crisis-rural-hospitals) looks at the other side of the supply question.

## What Have the Labor Department and Hospital Groups Said?

The department frames the change as protecting U.S. workers. Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said in the [department's announcement](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/eta/eta20261116) that the change "will ensure American workers are first in line."

The American Hospital Association said the plan "would exacerbate shortages of nurses and physical therapists," as [reported by Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/labor-department-schedule-foreign-nurses-green-card-proposal-481556). The long-term care association AHCA/NCAL plans to file comments. [Its notice](https://www.ahcancal.org/News-and-Communications/Blog/Pages/DOL-Proposes-Eliminating-Schedule-A-.aspx) reminds members that "This is a proposed rule, and Schedule A has not been eliminated."

## What to Do This Week

1. **Find out whether you sponsor anyone.** Ask HR or your recruiter which nurses are in a green card case, and which step each case has reached.
2. **Ask how cases already underway would be treated.** The proposed text does not clearly say what happens to a Schedule A petition filed before a final rule, so get your immigration attorney's reading.
3. **Check the layoff bar.** If your hospital laid off nurses at that site in the past two years, ask whether the proposed two-year bar would apply.
4. **Plan coverage for a longer vacancy.** Our guides to [the cost of losing a nurse at a rural hospital](/blog/cost-of-losing-a-nurse-rural-hospital) and [setting up a PRN pool](/blog/prn-pool-critical-access-hospital) cover how to staff a position that stays open longer.
5. **Comment if you have a view.** Comments go to regulations.gov under docket ETA-2026-0133 by January 19, 2027. The department invites "written comments on all aspects of this proposed rule."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can foreign-educated nurses still get green cards under the proposal?**

Yes. The proposal ends the Schedule A shortcut, and the green card itself stays available. A hospital would sponsor a nurse through PERM labor certification, with at least 90 days of recruiting and a Labor Department review.

**Q: Is the Schedule A change final?**

No. It is a proposed rule published on November 17, 2026, with comments open until January 19, 2027. The proposal describes a 60-day delayed effective date plus 30 calendar days of transition after a final rule is published.

**Q: How much longer would it take to hire a nurse from abroad?**

The paperwork before the green card petition would take about 14 to 17 months longer, by our math from Labor Department processing times. A nurse's place in the visa line is set before the Labor Department review, so by our reading the start date moves back about four to five months while visa waits stay this long.

**Q: Does the proposal add a wage determination?**

No. Schedule A cases already need a prevailing wage determination from the Labor Department. We did not check whether the proposal changes how the wage is set. The new steps are recruiting, the Labor Department's review and a layoff bar.
