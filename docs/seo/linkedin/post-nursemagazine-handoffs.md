## The post

> The 7am handoff is where misses get caught. It is rarely where they start.
>
> I contributed to a piece in Nurse Magazine on shift handoffs, and this was my argument.
>
> Most handoff advice is about the handoff. Better templates. Structured formats. One more checklist.
>
> But look at who is standing there at 7am.
>
> If the oncoming nurse has never worked that unit, or the outgoing one is on her third stretch in a row, no template saves that conversation. The gap was built into the schedule days earlier.
>
> The handoff is just where it becomes visible.
>
> So before you rewrite the form, pull the assignment pattern behind the last few misses. That is usually where the answer is.
>
> Full piece, with several other contributors: https://nursemagazine.co/qa/stronger-shift-handoffs-in-hospital-nursing-simple-moves-that-prevent-misses/

## Why it is written this way

**Leads with the argument, not the achievement.** "Excited to share" posts get
scrolled past. The claim that handoff problems start in the schedule is the only
part that makes someone stop, and it is also the only part that points back at
what we do.

**No product mention anywhere.** The byline on the article already says Founder,
SimpleScheduleAI, and the link is dofollow. Selling in the post would cost more
reach than it buys.

**Short lines on purpose.** LinkedIn truncates at roughly three lines before
"see more", so the hook and the setup both have to land above that fold.
