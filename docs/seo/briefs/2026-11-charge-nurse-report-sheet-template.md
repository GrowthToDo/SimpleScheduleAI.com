## 0. Verdict in five lines

1. **Demand is small and mostly not ours.** "charge nurse report sheet" = 110/mo (Google's figure; the 12-month mean of the monthly values is 122). The patient-level neighbours are 50x larger: "nurse report sheet" 5,400, "nurse brain sheet" 1,300.
2. **Intent is mixed, patient-roster-leaning.** Only 1 of the top 8 organic results for the primary keyword is a clearly unit-level form (the 2010 AJN hand-off PDF). The Etsy, Scribd and Pinterest results are multi-patient sheets. Details in section 2.
3. **Recommendation: write it only as a unit-level sheet, and say so in the first paragraph.** Name the difference from a patient-level "nurse brain sheet" and do not build a patient sheet. A patient sheet is patient documentation, out of scope for a scheduling company.
4. **Real gap, honestly ownable:** none of the five fully inspected unit-level templates has an on-call line, a call-in log, or an hours check before calling someone in. A small hospital needs all three.
5. **Do not expect traffic.** Expect tens of impressions a month. The value is a free, credible, citable resource that links to schedule posts we already have (see the "Content is NOT the acquisition channel" memory). Section 11 lists what should stop us.

## 4. Content of a real unit-level sheet (credible sources)

The field set should rest on these. Quote fragments are verbatim and under 15 words.

1. **AJN, the only clinical-journal charge hand-off form.** Flynn JP, Prufeta PA, Minghillo-Lipari L, "Cultivating quality: an evidence-based approach to taking charge," Am J Nurs 2010;110(9):58-63, PMID 20736714, DOI 10.1097/01.NAJ.0000388268.65499.bc. The online-only form (header text: 'Online-only content for "An Evidence-Based Approach to Taking Charge"') is at https://cdn-links.lww.com/permalink/ajn/a/ajn_110_9_2010_08_10_ajn_0_sdc1.pdf . Fields read from the PDF: Unit, Date, Shift, Charge Nurse, Staffing (RNs, PCTs/NAs), Sick Calls, Census, Admissions, Transfers, Discharges, OR, Quality Issues (Falls, Restraints, Pressure Ulcers, Unusual Incidents, Pyxis discrepancies, Isolation, Close Observations, Drips, Tele), Patient Family Needs/Concerns, Monitoring (Crash Cart / Defibrillator, Med / Specimen Refrigerators, Glucometers, EKG). The full column was not read (no abstract on PubMed; paywalled). **Do not reproduce or lightly re-skin the AJN form: it is Wolters Kluwer content. Cite it, describe the idea, build our own layout.** Also note it dates from 2010 and from an academic medical center, not a CAH.
2. **AHRQ PSNet, handoffs primer** (last reviewed June 15, 2024; WebFetch read 2026-11-02). Handoff: "the process of one healthcare provider updating another". "communication failures between providers have been found to be a leading cause". "Guidelines for safe handoffs focus on standardizing the process." URL: https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/handoffs-and-signouts . Use for why a fixed sheet beats memory. This is a general handoff source, not charge-specific.
3. **AHRQ TeamSTEPPS I-PASS** (https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/ipass.html): the page 403s fetchers; the structure (Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situation awareness and contingency planning, Synthesis by receiver) was confirmed only via search-result summaries. **Re-read in a browser before quoting.** Useful for one thing: a read-back line at the bottom of the sheet for the incoming charge nurse (the "S"). 4. **ANA** (dossier "Charge nurse: what the role is"): charge nurses "oversee the operations of their specific nursing unit during a set period", with duties including "Overseeing the smooth transition from one shift to another" and "Creating and evaluating staffing plans and schedules". The shift transition and staffing-plan lines are the authority for the sheet's two halves.
5. **American Nurse Journal, Bilyeu et al. 2023** (dossier): charge nurses coordinate "nurse-patient assignments"; "Many organizations lack role-specific competency-based orientation". Supports "give the new charge nurse a fixed page".
6. **Vendor templates (MangoApps, Process Street)** are secondary, undated, unreviewed: use only to show what the market lists, never as evidence of what is best practice. Not found, and not to be claimed: any nursing association, AHRQ, or CMS publication of a standard "charge nurse report sheet" field list. The AJN form is the closest. There is no source for "most charge nurses use a sheet".

What the Texas texts DO require that the sheet can reflect:

- **HSC 257.003(c)(2)**: the hospital shall "make readily available to nurses on each patient care unit" ... "at the beginning of each shift the official nurse services staffing plan levels and current staffing levels for that unit and that shift." (Verbatim from texas.public.law, verified there "May 26, 2025"; two fragments each under 15 words: "at the beginning of each shift" and "current staffing levels for that unit and that shift".)
- **26 TAC 505.41(o)(2)(G)(iii)** carries the same sentence for hospitals ("make readily available to nurses on each patient care unit at the beginning of each shift ...").
- **26 TAC 505.41(o)(2)(G)(ii)(II)**: the plan must "include a method for adjusting the staffing plan shift to shift" for each patient care unit.
- **26 TAC 505.41(o)(2)(G)(iv)**: the staffing committee's semiannual evaluation covers "variations between the staffing plan and actual staffing".
- **Caveat, HYPOTHESIS to settle with the compliance lead, not us:** neither text says who makes the levels available, on what medium, or that a sheet does it. We can say the sheet is one place to write the numbers down. We must not say it satisfies the rule. Test: ask one Texas CAH DON how they meet (c)(2) today.
- **HSC ch. 258 (dossier)**: "A hospital may not require a nurse to work mandatory overtime." §258.004(b): hospital "shall, to the extent possible, make a good faith effort" to cover through voluntary overtime, "including calling per diems and agency nurses, assigning floats, or requesting an additional day of work from off-duty employees." Prescheduled on-call and brief pre/post-shift handoff documentation time are outside mandatory overtime (dossier). Supports a call-in log that records who volunteered.
- **FLSA (dossier)**: use "the 40-hour weekly FLSA overtime threshold". FS#53: nurses staying past shift to chart are owed time (dossier, Example #13). A charge nurse finishing the report after the shift is working time. Use as a sentence in the handoff H2 only if it earns its place.

## 8. Template format recommendation

**Recommendation: one page, US Letter, printable PDF with fillable fields, black and white safe. Add nothing else on day one.**

Reasons, each from a check:

1. **The category ships PDFs.** AJN form: PDF. Etsy: PDF/Word/"editable fields" PDF. nursing.com: PDF set. NurseBrain: printable PDF. Plan Print Land: non-editable PDF. Excel appears only in examples.com's four XLS files, which are state cost-report forms and not nursing handoffs.
2. **The user is on the unit.** The AJN form, the MangoApps table and the Etsy packs are all paper-first. A 25-bed charge nurse at 6:45 a.m. hands over at a station, often on shared computers. (HYPOTHESIS about her desk; test = ask two Texas DONs.)
3. **No logins, no patient data in a file.** A PDF with unit counts only avoids the question of where a spreadsheet of census sits on a shared drive.
