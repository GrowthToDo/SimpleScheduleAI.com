## Part 2. Primary sources (verbatim)

Citation form for the post: 22 TAC 217.20, Tex. Occ. Code 303.005 / 303.0015 / 301.352 / 301.413. Reader links: Cornell LII for 217.20; FindLaw for statutes (not readable by me today; do not state "re-read on FindLaw" for these).

**F1. Rule text.** https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-217-20 (curl, 2026-11-16). LII history note: amended eff. 10/22/2019 (44 TexReg 6031). A rule-review notice for Title 22 Part 11 ran 2025-09-26 (WebSearch, UNVERIFIED whether it touches 217.20); re-check on publish day.

**F2. Who must offer it (217.20(c)(1))**: "TOC §303.0015 (NPR Law) requires a person who regularly employs, hires or contracts for the services of eight (8) or more nurses (for nursing peer review of an RN, at least four (4) of the 8 must be RNs) to permit a nurse to request safe harbor nursing peer review when the nurse is requested or assigned to engage in conduct that the nurse believes is in violation of his/her duty to a patient." NOTE: the threshold is 8 nurses with 4 RNs, NOT 10. Statute (curl, texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_303.0015): "(a) A person shall establish a nursing peer review committee ... for professional nurses, if the person regularly employs, hires, or contracts for the services of eight or more nurses, at least four of whom are registered nurses. (b) A person required to establish a nursing peer review committee under this section may contract with another entity to conduct the peer review for the person."

**F3. Definition (217.20(a)(15))**: "A process that protects a nurse from employer retaliation, suspension, termination, discipline, discrimination, and licensure sanction when a nurse makes a good faith request for nursing peer review of an assignment or conduct the nurse is requested to perform and that the nurse believes could result in a violation of the NPA or Board rules. Safe harbor must be invoked prior to engaging in the conduct or assignment for which nursing peer review is requested, and may be invoked at anytime during the work period when the initial assignment changes."

**F4. Duty to a patient (217.20(a)(5))**: "This includes administrative decisions directly affecting a nurse's ability to comply with that duty." (also 303.005(a), same phrase).

**F5. When and how (217.20(d)(1)-(2))**: invoked "(A) when the conduct is requested or assignment made; (B) when changes occur in the request or assignment that so modify the level of nursing care or supervision required ... that a nurse believes in good faith that patient harm may result; or (C) when the nurse refuses to engage in the requested conduct or assignment." "The nurse must notify the supervisor requesting the conduct or assignment in writing ... If a nurse is unable to complete a Safe Harbor Quick Request or other written form ... due to immediate patient care needs, the nurse may orally invoke safe harbor by notifying the nurse's supervisor of the request. A detailed written account ... must be completed before leaving the work setting at the end of the work period." (d)(2)(B): "After receiving oral notification of a request, the nurse's supervisor must record in writing the requirements described in paragraph (3) of this subsection, which must be signed and attested to by the requesting nurse and the nurse's supervisor who prepared the written record."

**F6. Quick Request contents (217.20(d)(3))**: "The BON Safe Harbor Quick Request Form may be used to initially invoke safe harbor, but use of the form is not required." Required items: nurse name and signature; date and time; location; "the name of the person who requested the nurse engage in the conduct or made the assignment"; "the name of the supervisor recording the request, if applicable"; "a brief explanation of why the nurse is requesting a nursing peer review committee determination"; "a description of the collaboration between the nurse and the supervisor, if applicable."

**F7. Comprehensive Written Request (217.20(d)(4))**: submitted "in writing before leaving the work setting at the end of the work period"; "The BON Comprehensive Written Request for Safe Harbor Nursing Peer Review Form may be used ... but use of the form is not required." Content includes the conduct assigned and who assigned it, practice setting ("resources available, extenuating or contributing circumstances"), the specific duty at risk, any rationale for not engaging pending determination, and documentation. (d)(5): "The nurse invoking safe harbor is responsible for keeping a copy of the request for safe harbor."

**F8. Statute on oral requests (303.005(b-1), (b-2))** (texas.public.law, curl): the supervisor "shall record in writing" seven items; "To be a valid request ... the written record ... must be signed and attested to by the requesting nurse and the nurse's supervisor who prepared the written record."

**F9. Protections (303.005(c); 217.20(e)(2))**: "A nurse who in good faith requests a peer review determination under Subsection (b): (1) may not be disciplined or discriminated against for making the request; (2) may engage in the requested conduct pending the peer review; (3) is not subject to the reporting requirement under Subchapter I, Chapter 301 (Nurses); and (4) may not be disciplined by the board for engaging in that conduct while the peer review is pending." 217.20(e)(2)(B): "A nurse or other person may not be suspended, terminated, or otherwise disciplined, retaliated, or discriminated against for advising a nurse in good faith of the nurse's right to request a determination." Board protection "remain[s] in place for 48 hours after the nurse is advised of the nursing peer review committee's determination," which "does not affect the nurse's protections from retaliation by the facility."

**F10. What is not protected (217.20(e)(4), (f)(1))**: "Safe harbor protections do not apply to any civil action for patient injury that may result from the nurse's practice." Board protection does not apply to "(A) the nurse who invokes safe harbor in bad faith; (B) conduct the nurse engages in prior to the request for safe harbor; or (C) conduct unrelated to the reason for which the nurse requested safe harbor." Bad faith (a)(2): "Knowingly or recklessly taking action not supported by a reasonable factual or legal basis." 303.005(a-1): good faith "if there is a reasonable factual or legal basis for the request or action." (j)(2): "A nurse who knowingly participates in nursing peer review in bad faith is subject to disciplinary action by the Board."

**F11. Nurse may keep working the assignment unless (217.20(g)(1))**: a nurse invoking "may engage in the requested conduct or assignment while awaiting nursing peer review determination unless" (A) the nurse "lacks the basic knowledge, skills, and abilities ... such that engaging in the requested conduct or assignment would expose one or more patients to an unjustifiable risk of harm" or (B) it "would constitute unprofessional conduct and/or criminal conduct such as fraud, theft, patient abuse, exploitation, or falsification." (g)(2): if the nurse refuses on scope grounds, "the nurse and supervisor must collaborate in an attempt to identify an acceptable assignment ... and the results of this collaborative effort must be documented in writing and maintained in nursing peer review records by the chair of the nursing peer review committee."

**F12. Committee due process (217.20(h)(2))**: the committee shall "comply with the membership and voting requirements as set forth in TOC §303.003"; "exclude from the committee membership, any persons or person with administrative authority for personnel decisions directly affecting the nurse"; limit "a CNO, nurse administrator, or other individual with administrative authority over the nurse, including the individual who requested the conduct or made the assignment, to appearing before the safe harbor nursing peer review committee to speak as a fact witness"; permit the nurse to "appear before the committee," "ask questions and respond," and "make a verbal and/or written statement." Membership (303.003): "A nursing peer review committee must have nurses as three-fourths of its members"; for RN practice, "registered nurses as two-thirds of its members" and only RNs vote; includes "to the extent feasible at least one nurse who has a working familiarity with the area of nursing practice."

**F13. Timelines (217.20(i))**: "(1) The safe harbor nursing peer review committee shall complete its review and notify the CNO or nurse administrator within 14 calendar days of when the nurse requested safe harbor. (2) Within 48 hours of receiving the committee's determination, the CNO or nurse administrator shall review these findings and notify the nurse requesting safe harbor of both the committee's determination and whether the administrator believes in good faith that the committee's findings are correct or incorrect." Employer policy duty (217.20(h)(1)): adopt "a policy to inform nurses of their right to request a nursing peer review committee determination (safe harbor nursing peer review) and the procedure for making a request." (303.005(i) same.)

**F14. CNO role (217.20(j)(1), (j)(4))**: the CNO or nurse administrator "is responsible for knowing the requirements of this Rule and for taking reasonable steps to assure that nursing peer review is implemented and conducted in compliance." If the nurse refused and the CNO "believes in good faith that the committee has incorrectly determined a nurse's duty," the determination is "not binding"; "the rationale for disagreeing must be recorded and retained with the nursing peer review records"; the committee's determination "shall be considered in any decision by the nurse's employer to discipline the nurse for the refusal." If the CNO "believes the nursing peer review was conducted in bad faith, she/he has a duty to report the nurses involved." "CNO" (a)(3): "The registered nurse, by any title, who is administratively responsible for the nursing services." Statute 303.005(d) matches ("not binding if a nurse administrator believes in good faith that the peer review committee has incorrectly determined a nurse's duty").

**F15. Informal workgroup (217.20(k))**: a facility "may choose to initiate an informal review process utilizing a workgroup of the nursing peer review committee provided that the final determination ... complies with the time lines" and written policies; the nurse must "consent, in writing," and may "reject any decision of the informal workgroup and have the entire committee determine." Chair must ratify.

**F16. Retaliation (303.005(h); 301.413)**: "A person may not suspend or terminate the employment of, or otherwise discipline or discriminate against, a nurse who in good faith requests a peer review determination under this section or a person who advises a nurse of the nurse's right." 301.413(b-1): a violator "may be reported to the appropriate licensing agency and ... that agency may impose an administrative penalty not to exceed $25,000"; 301.413(c): the nurse may recover "the greater of: (A) actual damages ... or (B) $5,000," exemplary damages, costs, fees; (d): a nurse whose employment is ended in violation is "entitled to reinstatement ... or severance pay in an amount equal to three months of the employee's most recent salary" plus lost wages. 303.005(f): "A nurse's rights under this section may not be nullified by a contract." (all texas.public.law, curl). 217.20(e)(3): "The BON does not have regulatory authority over practice settings or civil liability."

**F17. 301.352 (refusal)** (texas.public.law, curl): "(a-1) A nurse may refuse to engage in an act or omission relating to patient care that would constitute grounds for reporting the nurse to the board ..., that constitutes a minor incident, or that violates this chapter or a board rule if the nurse notifies the person at the time of the refusal that the reason for refusing is that the act or omission: (1) constitutes grounds for reporting the nurse to the board; or (2) is a violation of this chapter or a rule of the board." (b) the employer's act is not a violation if a peer review committee finds the refused act was not reportable/minor/violative, or if it finds it was and the person "rescinds any disciplinary or discriminatory action ..., compensates the nurse for lost wages; and restores ... lost benefits."

**F18. 217.19 vs 217.20 (distinguish only)**: 217.19(c) (curl LII): incident-based review applies where a person has eight or more nurses, "Any person or entity that conducts incident-based nursing peer review must comply with the due process requirements of this section even if the person or entity does not utilize the number of nurses described by subsection (c)." Incident-based = looks back at a nurse's conduct to decide whether it must be reported to the Board. Safe harbor = a nurse asks beforehand about an assignment. 217.20(a)(7): incident-based review "includes whether external factors beyond the nurse's control may have contributed to any deficiency in care."

**F19. BON Forms page** (curl/Wayback snapshot 2026-03-04, https://www.bon.texas.gov/forms_safe_harbor.asp.html): form names "BON Safe Harbor Quick Request Form", "BON Comprehensive Written Request for Safe Harbor Nursing Peer Review Form", "BON Safe Harbor Nursing Peer Review Resource for Nurses", "BON Safe Harbor Resources for Facilities". Quotes: "Safe harbor is a process between the nurse and the employer; please do not send these forms to the BON." "An entity required to have a nursing peer review plan must have policies and procedures that encompass, at a minimum, the requirements of the applicable statutes and rules ... it is not sufficient to use the Board's forms for safe harbor in place of official policies and procedures on nursing peer review." "A facility or other entity establishing a nursing peer review plan may create its own forms and procedures that comply with" Chapter 303 and rule 217.20. The Comprehensive form "includes a fill-in-the-blank format that the nursing peer review committee and chief nursing officer or nurse administrator can utilize to document the safe harbor nursing peer review process."

**F20. BON Resources for Facilities PDF** (curl/Wayback, https://www.bon.texas.gov/pdfs/safe_harbor_forms_pdfs/BONSafeHarborResourcesforFacilities.pdf, "Published 3/2020"; text pulled with my own PDF stream decoder, so only clean passages quoted): "The nurse's supervisor should promptly submit the Safe Harbor Quick Request and Comprehensive Written Request ... to the safe harbor nursing peer review committee chair because the safe harbor nursing peer review committee (Committee) must complete its review and notify the Chief Nursing Officer (CNO)/ nurse administrator [14] days of when the nurse invoked safe harbor, NOT 14 calendar days from when the Committee received the safe harbor forms. Thus, any delay in providing the forms to the nursing peer review committee chair will unnecessarily decrease the number of days the Committee has to meet and make a determination." (the "[14]" digits were garbled by extraction; the rule F13 is the clean source). The same PDF carries the committee chair's eight-item due-process attestation and the CNO 48-hour sign-off. "use of this document is not required by a BON law or rule."

**F21. BON FAQ, Nursing Peer Review** (curl/Wayback snapshot 2026-01-12, https://www.bon.texas.gov/faq_peer_review.asp.html, safe harbor section "Updated 2022"):
- Staffing is named: "Examples of safe harbor situations include clinical assignments related to staffing and/or acuity of patients when the nurse believes patient harm may result [Tex. Admin. Code §217.11(1)(B) and (T)]".
- "A request to accept an assignment when a nurse believes the nurse staffing levels are unsafe would be conduct a nurse normally would engage in pending nursing peer review's determination since the supervisor normally would have some reasonable legal or factual basis to support her/his belief that the requested assignment does not violate a nurse's duty to a patient, even if nursing peer review ultimately determines otherwise."
- "The nurse's request for safe harbor nursing peer review does not become invalid and the nurse does not have to withdraw his/her request for safe harbor when a supervisor is able to respond with adequate staff, equipment or other resources. It is the nurse's decision whether or not he/she wishes to have a nursing peer review of the situation."
- "The ability to invoke safe harbor protections and to have a safe harbor nursing peer review committee evaluate the requested assignment are the same whether the nurse accepts or refuses the assignment in good faith."
- "Invoking safe harbor has no effect on a nurse's civil or criminal liability ... There is no expiration of the protection against retaliatory actions such as demotion, forced change of shifts, pay cut, or other retaliatory actions against the nurse."
- "The BON encourages CNOs/Nurse Administrators to remember that each nurse has a duty to advocate for patient/client safety."
- Committee: "A CNO, nurse administrator, or other individual with administrative authority over the nurse, including the individual who requested the conduct or made the assignment ..., may only appear before the committee to speak as a fact witness."
- Threshold: "for professional nurses ...: if the person regularly employs, hires, or contracts for the services of eight (8) or more nurses, at least four (4) of whom are registered nurses." The BON General Information PDF (curl/Wayback, "Published 3/2020") says the committee requirement "is based upon the number of nurses regularly employed, hired, or contracted."

**F22. 217.11 standards** (curl LII): (1)(B) "Implement measures to promote a safe environment for clients and others"; (1)(T) "Accept only those nursing assignments that take into consideration client safety and that are commensurate with the nurse's educational preparation, experience, knowledge, and physical and emotional ability"; (1)(K) "Comply with mandatory reporting requirements of Texas Occupations Code Chapter 301." BON Position Statement 15.14, "Duty of a Nurse in any Practice Setting" (curl/Wayback snapshot 2026-02-07, https://www.bon.texas.gov/practice_bon_position_statements_content.asp.html#15.14): "a nurse has a duty to the patient which cannot be superseded by hospital policy or physician's order" (Lunsford v. Board of Nurse Examiners, 648 S.W. 2d 391; the statement describes a rural hospital chest-pain case).

**F23. CAH / small facility applicability.** No provision names Critical Access Hospitals, and none reads as a CAH carve-out or CAH rule (checked: 217.20, 217.19(c), 303.0015, 303.005, BON FAQ). Applicability turns only on the head count in 303.0015: 8 or more nurses regularly employed, hired or contracted, with 4 RNs for RN review. A CAH with 10 to 40 nurses is over the line on the head count (arithmetic, not a source claim). What happens to a nurse at an employer under eight is NOT ANSWERED in the sources read. 301.352 protects a refusing nurse against "a person" with no head-count threshold, but the sources do not say how a committee determination is obtained without a committee. Physicians Practice (WebFetch, attorney commentary) says employers under eight "don't qualify"; treat as secondary. Hospital-wide: whether the CAH is staffed by contract agency nurses counts toward the eight is supported by the statutory words "hires, or contracts for", not by any BON example.

**F24. Health & Safety Code ch. 257 (staffing committee).** 257.003 and 257.004 (texas.public.law, curl, 2026-11-16) contain NO mention of safe harbor, peer review or Chapter 303. The only retaliation language is 257.003(a)(5): "protecting from retaliation nurses who provide input to the committee." Do not connect ch. 257 to Safe Harbor beyond "separate processes." ## Claims we must NOT make
- That SimpleScheduleAI or any software handles, tracks, routes or documents Safe Harbor, or that scheduling "solves" the staffing problems behind it.
- "10 nurses" (the threshold is eight, four of them RNs) or that CAHs are exempt or specially covered.
- That only Texas and New Mexico have safe harbor (WebSearch/Nursa/vendor claim, UNVERIFIED; not needed).
- That the BON forms are mandatory, or that requests go to the BON (they do not).
- That the committee decides binding outcomes (the CNO may disagree in good faith; F14) or that the DON can deny or block a request (F16: retaliation and blocking exposure).
- That safe harbor protects against civil liability, or protects a nurse who invoked in bad faith, or protects conduct before the request.
- That a nurse who refuses an unsafe-staffing assignment is automatically protected from discipline: BON says a nurse "normally would engage" while the review runs (F21); refusal protection runs through 301.352 and the committee outcome.
- "14 business days" or "14 days from receipt" (it is 14 calendar days from invocation).
- Any frequency, rate or "most common" claim about how often nurses invoke (no source read).
- That ch. 257 or ch. 258 contains a safe harbor provision (F24).
- That 303.006/.007 confidentiality text was read (only cited through 217.20(j)(3)).
- Pilot customers, outcomes or invented hospital anecdotes. A worked example must be labelled illustrative.
