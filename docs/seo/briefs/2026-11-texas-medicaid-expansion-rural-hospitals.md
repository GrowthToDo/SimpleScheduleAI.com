**Plain-English summary the article can carry (all derived, labelled):** under an ordinary bill the law could not take effect before August 30, 2027. Add CMS's up-to-90-day review and the state's own build time (North Carolina and South Dakota each took roughly eight months from authority to coverage), and the first newly covered patient walks into a Texas critical access hospital no earlier than late 2027 on the fastest track the rules allow, and plausibly later. A two-thirds vote for immediate effect is the only route the constitution offers to an earlier date. This is a calendar, not a forecast that any bill passes.

**The hook (citable, ours):** the hospital studies everyone quotes measured expansions without these rules. Lindrooth et al. used 2008-16 data (verified, 4a). Any Texas expansion now arrives with work checks at application, six-month renewals and only one month of retroactive coverage (3c, 3h, 3i), and expanding would also move Texas onto the lower provider-tax and directed-payment ceilings that P.L. 119-21 set for expansion states (3l, 3m). For a CAH business office the operative detail is 3i: an uninsured patient admitted today could, under a new Texas expansion, be covered back only to the first day of the month before they apply, not three months. Keep this framed as "what the rules say", not "what will happen to your margin".
