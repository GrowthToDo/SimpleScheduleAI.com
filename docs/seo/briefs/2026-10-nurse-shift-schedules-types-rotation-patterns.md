**Hook (our arithmetic, script-checked): one nurse over 8 weeks, Monday-to-Sunday workweek, overtime after 40 hours.**

| Pattern | Shift | Average hours a week | Busiest week | Overtime hours in 8 weeks | Weekends with a shift, of 8 | Most shifts in a row |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed Monday to Friday | 8-hour | 40 | 40 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| Every other weekend, 9 shifts per 2 weeks | 8-hour | 36 | 40 | 0 | 4 | 3 |
| Fixed three weekdays | 12-hour | 36 | 36 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| Three a week, every other weekend | 12-hour | 36 | 36 | 0 | 4 | 3 |
| 2-2-3 | 12-hour | 42 | 60 | 80 | 4 | 3 |
| 4 on, 4 off | 12-hour | 42 | 48 | 32 | 5 | 4 |

The finding comes from a brute force over every 14-day layout. An 8-hour nurse on every other weekend at 40 hours EVERY week must work 7 shifts in a row. The alternative is a 32-hour or 48-hour week. A 12-hour nurse gets the same weekends at 36 hours with at most 3 in a row. The 2-2-3 and 4-on-4-off rows match the 12-hour post (T10). The 4-on-4-off row holds for any start day. Call the pattern "2-2-3", because sources disagree on which one Pitman and Panama mean.
