# Research brief: DOL proposal to end Schedule A for nurses (Newsroom, /articles)

Prepared 2026-11-25 (all "date read" values below are 2026-11-25). Status: HYPOTHESIS, key quotes to be re-checked by the main session before drafting. Method: `.claude/skills/trendjacking-articles.md`.

## The story in 3 lines

1. On 2026-11-17 the Labor Department published a proposed rule (91 FR 73916) that would delete Schedule A, the list that lets employers sponsoring foreign-educated registered nurses and physical therapists skip the PERM recruitment test. It is a proposal. Comments close 2027-01-19. Nothing has changed yet.
2. If finalized, a hospital would first prove it cannot find a U.S. nurse (at least 90 calendar days of recruitment), then wait for a DOL analyst review that currently averages 300 to 360 days, before it can even file the I-140. The nurse would still be eligible for an employment-based green card, and still waits for a visa number on top.
3. Gap on the SERP: nobody writes what a longer wait means for a small rural hospital's roster. Texas data (TCNWS, 2023) shows internationally educated RNs are 9.1% of Texas RNs but only 4.7% to 5.6% in the Panhandle, East, South and West Texas regions, so claims that small Texas hospitals depend on this pipeline are NOT supported by data we found.

### Possible sharp reframe (HYPOTHESIS, needs an immigration attorney before it goes in the article)

The priority date, which is what the visa-number line is measured from, is set when DOL accepts the PERM application (8 CFR 204.5(d)), which happens after recruitment but BEFORE the roughly 10-to-12-month analyst review. Under Schedule A the priority date is the I-140 filing date, right after the wage determination. So the nurse's place in line moves back by about the recruitment period (4 to 5 months), not by recruitment plus review. If a nurse would wait for a visa number longer than the DOL review anyway, the review time overlaps with the queue and the delay in the nurse actually arriving is closer to the recruitment time than to 14 to 17 months. The test that settles it: an immigration attorney confirms the rule, and the official Visa Bulletin shows how long a fresh priority date waits. Do NOT publish this as a finding. If confirmed it is the strongest citable hook, because every competitor says "adds more than a year".

## Wrong, never write

- Mixing data years: TCNWS is 2023, NCSBN is calendar 2025, the FLAG page is as of 10/31/2026, Visa Bulletin is the November 2026 bulletin, DOL petitions are FY 2023 to 2025. Never blend the 2020 Texas BON 9.6% figure with the 2023 9.1%.
