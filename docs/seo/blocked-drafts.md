# Blocked drafts

Drafts that cannot be published by running the normal pipeline phases, because
something in the piece is wrong in a way a gate will not catch and a find/replace
will not fix. The publish-pipeline skill points here; keep this file short and
**delete rows once they are resolved**, so it stays a live list rather than a log.

The honest ED angle is NOT credentials: it is that ED callouts need a confirmed replacement fast, and what we actually enforce is role, competency level, charge coverage and overtime headroom. Do not reuse the ICU fix here, because no ED-specific rule exists in the engine. |
| `december-nurse-callout-surge-cah` | Built on a fabricated central statistic. "Critical Access Hospitals see nurse callout rates rise 25-35% between Thanksgiving and New Year's Day", attributed to "scheduling data reported by CAH administrators", appears in the excerpt, a Key Takeaway and the FAQ. **Research pass 2026-09-22 traced it to origin: no such figure exists.** The only percentage anywhere on the indexed web is "up to 30%" from an uncited NurseRegistry staffing-agency blog post, laundered onward by HelpSquad. No peer-reviewed study, BLS series, CDC/NIOSH dataset, NSI, Press Ganey or hospital-association report gives any holiday callout percentage for US hospital nurses. No CAH callout dataset exists from CMS, the Flex Monitoring Team, NRHA or Texas DSHS, so the attribution describes a source that cannot be produced, and we have zero customers. Two inline `<svg>` charts also plot invented callout and volume curves. Plus a banned product claim: the callout shortlist "filtered by ... certification match" (inventory: `certifications` is a `string[]` **not consulted by any rule**). | Cut the 25-35% figure entirely rather than softening it: softening still launders an agency's uncited marketing number into a YMYL claim. Delete both SVGs. **Also cut, verified unsupported:** "the two weeks following Thanksgiving are consistently higher than Thanksgiving week" (NOT FOUND, zero sources) and "December 27-30 is the lowest patient volume of the year". **One draft premise is actively contradicted:** December elective volume is *higher*, not lower. Piersa et al., *Anesthesiology* 2021;135(5):804-812 (PMID 34525169), 3.5M adult cases across Florida and Texas, found "a 20% increase over the January-to-November baseline", deductible-driven. **Usable real sources if rewritten:** Dexter/Epstein, *Perioper Care Oper Room Manag* 2020;21:100139 (PMID 33102816) found days adjacent to holidays carry 1.45x the unscheduled-absence rate of midweek days, in anesthesia staff not floor nurses; CDC FluView for respiratory season (note the peak is Dec-Feb and fell in **February** in 2024-25, so do not write "December"); CPSC holiday decorating injuries. **The CFR cite is wrong three ways:** the draft says "Texas DSHS regulations require CAHs to maintain RN coverage at all times under CMS §485.635(c)". Correct section is **§485.631(a)(5)**, verbatim "A registered nurse, clinical nurse specialist, or licensed practical nurse is on duty whenever the CAH has one or more inpatients" — so RN **or** CNS **or** LPN, and only when inpatients are present, not "at all times". §485.635(c) is about outside service agreements. It is also a federal CMS Condition of Participation, not a Texas DSHS rule. **Dead link:** `dshs.texas.gov/acute-settlement-agreement` is unindexed and concerns settlements, not staffing. Replace with the DSHS 2022 Hospital Nurse Staffing Study, Rural and Critical Access Hospitals. **Genuine Texas angle available:** Health & Safety Code §257.004 requires every hospital licensed under Chapter 241, CAHs included with no exemption, to run a nurse staffing committee meeting at least quarterly, 60% direct-care RNs. Texas mandates a committee and a plan, not a ratio. |
