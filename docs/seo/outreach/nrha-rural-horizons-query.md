## Why this is a query and not a pitch

Two reasons, and they pull in the same direction.

**We do not know if they take vendor-founder pieces.** The magazines page
publishes an editorial address and separates advertising into a different
contact entirely, and it states no membership restriction. But silence is not
permission. The NRHA *blog* (P3 target 1) states its restriction verbatim and was
disqualified on that basis, so the parent organisation demonstrably gates some
surfaces and not others.

**Target 1 already taught us the cost of guessing.** Verifying the route first
cost one page fetch and removed the top-ranked target before a word of pitch copy
was written. A three-line email is the same trade: cheap, and it prevents writing
a 1,500-word feature for a closed door.

Note the contrast with Chief Healthcare Executive, where a query would be WRONG:
their guidance explicitly says send the finished piece, not a pitch. The rule is
not "always query" or "always send". It is "do what the outlet's own page says",
and when the page says nothing, ask.

## Timing

Hold until CHE resolves. The lane rule is one outlet at a time, judged on
placements rather than volume sent. CHE went out 2026-08-18 and is the best-fit
target found so far.

## Why it is written this way

**The vendor disclosure is in the second paragraph, not buried.** Every P1 and P2
email in this programme discloses, and it has never cost us a reply. It did cost
the RHIhub submission, which was declined, and that decline was correct and
predictable rather than a loss of standing.

**It asks one question and offers an easy no.** An editor can answer in one line.
That is the whole point of a query at this stage.

**It does not pitch the idea.** If they say yes, the angle gets developed then,
against whatever their worksheet requires. Pitching an angle now would waste the
reply on a topic they may not be able to accept anyway.
