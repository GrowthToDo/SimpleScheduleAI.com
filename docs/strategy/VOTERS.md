## The verdict

**Nothing is special-and-extreme yet. This is a zero-voter file.**

### Why, stated honestly

An earlier draft argued this from "pre-launch, zero customers, so test 3 is
unpassable." That reasoning is derivable from a single input fact, which means it
would produce the same verdict for **any** pre-launch company and says nothing
about SimpleScheduleAI specifically. It also picked the strictest reading of test
3 while ignoring the clause that does apply here: the method lists
**walked-away-from segments** as valid decisiveness evidence, and those do not
require customers.

The honest version: test 3 as usually read is unpassable at this stage, so the
verdict is partly a property of the stage. What this file CAN do is rank
candidates on the tests that are testable now, which are extremity evidence and
segments already refused. That ranking is below, and it is the real work.

The candidate that reached furthest — the plate-clearing commitment — fails on
its own merits and not merely on stage: its rarity claim is **false** (see
below), and its reverberation holds in only one of the two modes we ship.

### The response: one decision, one number

The method's response is to invest the deepest genuine obsession into extremity.
For this company that reduces to a single question that can be answered on
Monday, and an earlier draft dodged it in favour of copy-editing instructions.

**Does the self-serve `/product` tier live or die?**

If the bet is "the hospital operates nothing," a live self-serve tier where the
hospital operates everything is the bet's direct negation. `/product` is built,
priced per user, carries a shipped nurse portal, is submitted to software
directories, and is hidden from the sitemap. That is not a refusal, it is a hedge
with the lights off, and maintaining two positioning columns costs hours that
would otherwise go into making one of them extreme. Kill it or commit to it, and
either answer is defensible. Running both is what prevents a voter from forming.

**Second, name the guarantee.** "Turnaround guaranteed rather than typical"
appeared in an earlier draft with no number and no penalty, which is a promise
rather than a guarantee. The canon to change already exists (onboarding 3 to 5
business days; first schedule inside two weeks; escalation 1 hour in business
hours, 3 after). A guarantee needs a stated remedy when it is missed.

## Special strengths (near-miss voters — deploy as secondary pillars)

### The plate-clearing commitment

**The hospital operates nothing: the scheduling work leaves the nurse manager's
plate entirely, so she can go back to patient care.**

Founder, unprompted, 2026-08-15: "The idea behind service is to take it off their
plates entirely so that they can focus on the work they like, patient care. Even
if we make it a product, the idea remains the same."

- **Extremity** — PARTIAL, PROVISIONAL. The founder was candid that this is about
  a year of intense work, not a long obsession. An earlier draft called the
  service model a "structural sacrifice"; that is wrong. For a solo, pre-launch,
  zero-brand founder, self-serve software is not the easier path available and
  declined, it is the path that does not work yet, because self-serve needs
  brand, support and integrations that do not exist. Doing the work by hand is
  the standard cold-start move. It becomes a sacrifice at the first buyer who
  wants to self-serve and is turned away.
- **Rarity** — **FALSE AS PREVIOUSLY WRITTEN, now UNTESTED.** An earlier draft
  said "every tracked competitor sells software the hospital operates." Our own
  competitor dossier (line 1058) records YouShift's differentiator verbatim: *"if
  needed, our team manages your scheduling end-to-end, removing the
  administrative burden from your organization"*, plus *"Our implementation team
  handles rule configuration, data migration, and initial schedule generation."*
  The founder independently confirmed the same thing on the same day: YouShift is
  doing something similar for clinical teams.
  **The deeper problem is that our evidence base cannot test this claim at all.**
  The dossier's inclusion criterion is "competitors covered across all blog
  posts", and those posts are software comparisons organised around Capterra and
  G2 product IDs. Staffing agencies, MSPs, VMS vendors, per-diem desks and
  scheduling outsourcers could never have entered that file. Rarity of a service
  model cannot be established from a software-vendor census.
  **Open task before this bet is priced: profile five non-software providers**
  (agency-managed scheduling, central staffing offices, healthcare BPO) and
  record what they charge and what they do.
- **Decisiveness** — UNTESTED with customers, but segments genuinely refused:
  every hospital over 25 beds, 49 states, and every buyer needing payroll or HRIS
  connections. Those are real walk-aways and they count under the method's own
  wording. What has NOT happened is any buyer accepting a cost for the trait.
  - *No nurse-facing app* — and this is a stronger refusal than an earlier draft
    claimed. The nurse portal is BUILT (`/my`, availability, leave, swaps,
    notifications) and deliberately suppressed in service copy. That is a policy
    refusal of a shipped capability, not an absence.
  - *Manual Excel hand-off instead of integration.* (Corrected from "no
    integration": schedule, roster and audit exports do exist.)
- **Reverberates** — **PARTIAL, SERVICE MODE ONLY.** An earlier draft called this
  a hard pass on the strength of six forced decisions. Three of them bend the
  other way in product mode, which ships today: the nurse portal exists, the
  scheduling team is replaced by "21 automated rule checks", and pricing is $10
  per user per month rather than per facility. The architecture did not bend one
  way; it was built both ways and one branch was hidden. What holds in BOTH
  modes: Excel upload instead of integration, and approval outside the app.
  (Also corrected: service pricing is not "flat per facility" — it is banded by
  roster size, $1,000 up to 20 nurses and $1,500 for 21 to 40.)
- **Energizes** — PROVISIONAL, self-reported, offered unprompted.

### The publish gate that blocks the founder

**A machine that makes backsliding on claims mechanically impossible.**

This replaces a candidate previously titled "refusing to overclaim the product",
which was a virtue-word and died at the gauntlet. No competitor announces that it
keeps claims it knows are false, so the trait as titled discriminated nothing.
Per the method, the word is cut and the evidence transferred to the specific,
citable fact underneath.

- **The artifact** — `check-blog.mjs` (58KB of enforced rules), `publish-gate.mjs`
  with a 13-row manifest, `check-links.mjs`, a pre-commit hook that blocks the
  founder's own `draft: false` flip when a row is red, `GOVERNANCE.md`'s doc
  authority hierarchy, and a 60-day staleness rule on competitor quotes. That is
  a governance regime built by a company with zero customers.
- **Extremity** — REAL, and better evidenced than the plate-clearing candidate.
- **Rarity** — genuinely unusual in this vendor space.
- **Reverberates** — YES, and an earlier draft never ran this test. It forces
  what can ship, gates every publish, and has repeatedly overruled the founder's
  own intent mid-session.
- **Energizes** — untested; the machinery is maintained, which is weak evidence
  it is not resented.
- **Missing axis** — no customer has eaten any cost for it, and it is invisible
  from outside the company.
- **The honest ledger, which an earlier draft suppressed.** The machine exists
  BECAUSE claims shipped. The credential-tracking ban was set 2026-06-23 and was
  still live in 51 places across 23 files on 2026-08-12, seven weeks later, found
  by accident during an unrelated review. `credibility-audit-live-posts.md` still
  records 164 flagged lines across 29 live files with exactly one marked
  resolved. "Systematically removes" was too strong; the accurate claim is that
  the company built enforcement after discovering it could not trust itself, and
  the remediation is partial.

## Shaping strengths (true, useful, not voters)

- **Researched knowledge of the space** — research papers, Reddit, competitor
  review mining, 30+ nurse-manager interviews before building. Real and
  above-average, but this is what good customer discovery IS, and a well-informed
  peer would not today name this founder as THE person for CAH nurse scheduling.
- **Extreme market narrowness** — Texas, Critical Access, 25 beds or fewer.
  Shapes everything; vertical narrowness is a common strategy.
- **Roster-size-banded pricing** — a real differentiator against per-seat, and
  copyable on a Tuesday.
- **The scheduling engine** — three ranked drafts, hard constraints, weekend and
  holiday fairness across periods. Every serious competitor has an engine.
- **Founder hospital-operations background** — the founder scoped this down
  himself: "only a very small part of the experience which I have now" comes from
  Apollo.
