## How to read this file

**The N-numbers are identifiers, not rankings.** They are assigned in the order
each level was settled, then frozen, so a positioning line citing "N3" keeps
meaning the same thing when a level is inserted later. Levels added by the
adversarial pass therefore carry high numbers at low positions.

**The ladder branches near the top.** An earlier draft forced a single column and
the adversarial pass showed that two pairs are siblings rather than rungs. The
diagram is the shape; the sections below carry the detail.

## The stack

### Purpose levels (siblings, not stacked)

**N6.** ★ **"Be the one who held this unit together."** — *purpose level: no
product satisfies this directly; material for higher-purpose stories, never for
promises.*

**N7.** ★ **"Have a life outside the hospital again."** — *purpose level.* This is
why "Sunday night" works as a phrase: the schedule eats the part of her week that
was supposed to be hers.

These two are **siblings, not a ladder**. The adversarial pass ran the test both
ways: satisfy N7 fully and she still wants N6; satisfy N6 fully and she still
wants N7. Neither is a means to the other, and stacking them was padding. Note
the tension between them, which is real and worth respecting in any story we
tell: being the indispensable manager is exactly what eats the weekends.

### Aspiration levels (siblings)

**N9.** → **"Nobody gets hurt on my watch."**
Occupants: staffing-ratio regulation and the Texas HSC 257 committee route;
accreditation and CMS survey processes; acuity tools; incident-reporting and
patient-safety programs.
Role: **aspiration** — reference it, never promise it. This is a patient-safety
claim, which is the highest-risk category of overclaim we have.
Added by the adversarial pass, which found that a seven-level ladder for a nurse
leader mentioned no patient anywhere. It is the reason "someone who can *actually
do it*" is in the promise wording at all, and it is the answer she would give
first in an interview.

**N5.** → **"Keep my nurses from quitting."**
Occupants: pay rises and sign-on bonuses; career ladders; tuition support;
culture work; recruiting and retention consultants.
Role: **aspiration** — reference it, never promise it.
Why our level still wins for her: every occupant above costs real budget and
takes quarters to show anything, where the fairness constraint is applied in the
same run as coverage rather than bought separately.

N9 and N5 are siblings, not stacked: satisfy either fully and she still wants the
other.

### The promise

**N3.** → **"Know every shift is covered, without leaning on the same three
people again."**
Occupants: agency and travel staffing firms; float pools and internal resource
teams; per diem agencies; overtime; and the scheduling platforms, which sell
coverage and fill-rate too (Smart Square, QGenda, ShiftWizard all market at this
level, not only at N1).
Role: **the promise** — the outcome we claim as a consequence of N1.

**This level was merged from two.** An earlier draft had coverage as the promise
and "stop burning out the same few nurses" as a rung above it. The adversarial
pass killed that: you can cover every shift *by* hammering three willing nurses,
so coverage does not entail fairness, and she does not pursue coverage *in order
to* be fair. They are two outputs of one decision. Merging them also puts our
actual differentiation inside the promise, since the fairness constraint is the
thing that separates our version of coverage from an agency booking.

What we may honestly say here: our drafts distribute weekends and holidays
against a running per-nurse count, with weekend history carried across periods,
and the callout shortlist sorts straight-time before overtime. Those are
mechanisms, stated in hours and in ordering, never in dollars.

**N8.** → **"My nurses know when they're working, far enough ahead to plan their
lives."**
Occupants: self-scheduling initiatives; posted-schedule policies and union or
committee rules on advance notice; shift-swap apps.
Role: **a second promise, sibling to N3** — the outcome of the *publication* half
of N1.
Added by the adversarial pass, which showed N1 bundles two jobs with different
parents: patching holes leads to coverage, but getting the schedule *out* leads
to predictability. Guarantee perfect coverage with nothing ever posted and the
unit still collapses. We genuinely serve this: the delivery cadence is a
post-ready schedule every Thursday.

### What we do

**N1.** → **"Get the schedule out every cycle, and patch the holes when people
call out."** ← YOU ARE HERE
Occupants: Excel and paper; the incumbent platforms (ShiftWizard, QGenda, symplr
Smart Square, UKG, TCP/Aladtec, NurseGrid); the newer AI entrants (M7 Health,
In-House Health); SimpleScheduleAI.
Role: **what you do** — features live here; meet searchers where they are.

Phrasing note: the founder supplied the vocabulary. "Stop rebuilding the schedule
every Sunday night" is the line the founder uses, drawn from the pre-build
interviews, and "patching holes" is the managers' own phrase for covering
callouts. (An earlier draft claimed this line is "what these managers respond
to". That is a message-testing claim and nothing has been tested; downgraded.)

### What we take over

**N2.** → **"Know who's available, who's off, and who I can actually call."**
Occupants: the Excel roster she already keeps; HR and HRIS exports; a paper
binder or whiteboard at the nurses' station; the roster modules inside the
scheduling platforms; NurseGrid on the nurse-facing side.
Role: **advancement — we take over maintaining it; she still supplies the weekly
inputs.** Per the positioning registry's `weekly-inputs` row she still sends PTO,
known constraints and ED spikes every week, so "we make this obsolete" was an
overclaim and has been corrected.

Founder note (2026-08-15): if this level is not handled for her, she does the
whole schedule manually **while also working the floor**. Her scheduling hours are
not free time; they are taken from clinical work.

## The real competitive picture

An earlier draft concluded that our real alternative is agency spend rather than
QGenda or ShiftWizard. **That conclusion has been removed as false**, and the
reasoning is recorded here because the error is instructive.

It rested on the claim that a DON who calls an agency "has satisfied N3 and never
needs N1 at all." She has covered one shift. The rest of the cycle still needs
building, so she plainly still needs N1. Beyond that: agency solves a **supply**
problem (no nurse exists to call), we solve an **allocation** problem (nurses
exist, arranged badly), which makes them complements rather than substitutes; the
platforms do market at N3, so the occupant lists overlap; and the persona this
stack is built on does not control the contract-labor budget in the first place.

What the occupant lists actually license:

- **Against the platforms** we compete at N1 for the same job, which is what the
  comparison corpus already does correctly.
- **Against the labor sources** we do not substitute. We change the order in
  which they get used, claimable in hours and in ordering, never in dollars.
- **The default alternative in a real deal is Excel and her Sunday night**, the
  do-nothing incumbent sitting at N1.

Note also that a "2 to 3× base hourly rate" figure appeared in an earlier draft.
It is **not in the facts dossier**; its only source in this repo is a comment in
`scoring.ts` justifying a penalty weight. It has been struck. Do not reintroduce
it without a sourced dossier entry.

## Uses beyond positioning

Measure success **one level up** from what we deliver: not "did the schedule get
built" but whether shifts were covered without the same three names absorbing
them. The purpose levels (N6, N7) are where the company's higher purpose lives
and where the only true stories worth telling on the site come from. Moving the
product up a level is a different business; the realistic climbs are content and
features that help with N3, N8 and N9, not a repositioning onto them.
